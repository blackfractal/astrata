# Astrata: Game Design Document v4.140
*(working title through v4.22 was Arcane Architect. That phrase is retired and must not appear anywhere in the game, to stay clear of the Architect in Slay the Spire 2. The title is Astrata, with no subtitle.)*
## Roguelike Deckbuilder with a Spatial Spell Grid

---

## 1. Core Concept
*Astrata* is a roguelike deckbuilder in two layers:

* **The Strata (11x11):** a field where the player travels, and where encounters, loot, and taverns spawn. No combat happens here.
* **The Mind Grid (7x6):** the player's spell/card layout during battles with enemies.  In battles, the cycle is: sight, placement, activation, enemy turn.  In sight, the player draws cards from the grimoire / deck.  In placement, the player chooses from those drawn cards and places them onto the grid. In activation, the players activate cards that have been placed on the grid.  Card effects come from *where* cards have been placed in the grid in relation to other cards, and their activation effects.  Some cards additionally (rarely) have effects when they are seen / drawn, and/or when they are placed.

The player grows along three economies during battle: how many cards they **see** in one turn, how many they can **place** in one turn, and how many they can **activate** in one turn. Decks may specialize in one or more of the three, or ignore all three in favor of raw damage.  Most successful decks require all of the above.

### 1.1 Lore
The story is told in three layers. The player is handed the first, earns the second by playing, and may never see the third except through strong exploratory, repeated play.

**Layer one: what the player is told at the start.**
The world of Astrata is being terrorized by the **Apex Predator**. The player **journeys** through the Strata, the layers of that world, to hunt it. The journey is never up or down: Strata are not higher or lower than one another, each is simply **deeper** and **more complex** than the last. Each Stratum is a Field to cross and a gatekeeper Archon to defeat; every battle is fought on the Mind Grid, the player's own inner workspace. Kill the Apex on Strata 4 and the run is won.

**Layer two: what the player comes to understand by the end of a run.**
The Apex Predator is **feeding** on the world Astrata itself. The deeper the Stratum, the more the world seems to nourish it, and killing it never feels final, it is always revived.

**Layer three: the hidden truth.**
Astrata is not a place. She is a conscious, living being, and everything the player meets is happening inside her. The feeding is **deliberate**. Astrata empowers the Apex Predator on purpose, and she **resurrects it every time the player kills it**, which is why the game is played again and again. She believes that all other life within her is a plague on her own existence, and she is using the Apex Predator, the Motes, the Eidolons, and the Archons to scour out every last "messy" living thing. It is a body taking antibiotics to kill everything inside it, forgetting that it needs some of what it is killing. Her goal is pathological to herself, and the player is one of the things she desires to eradicate.

Under that reading, the mechanics are her own overactive defenses. Motes, Eidolons, and Archons are her immune response, the Probability Peak at the True Center is her attention, Restlessness is that attention gathering, Hunters are the response she sends once she has decided, and Curses and Hexes are her reaching into the player's belongings and mind. Taverns are pockets she cannot see into, where other inhabitants shelter. Deeper Strata are closer to the core of her thinking, which is why they are more complex and why the Apex is strongest there.

**Where the layers show.** Layer one is the setting screen at the start of a run and the surface of every Event. Layer two is carried by Event text and enemy descriptions across Strata 2 through 4, never as a statement, only as a pattern the player notices. Layer three is never stated. It is there to be pieced together from secrets, the way Five Nights at Freddy's hides a deeper story under a simple one, and future versions will add the secrets that reveal it. The only crack in the surface is the last line of a winning run: after the results screen for defeating the Apex, in small lettering, **"... but Astrata is still restless ..."**

**A breadcrumb for later.** Because run history persists (10.10), a future secret can count how many times the Apex Predator has died across the player's runs and let something in the world react to that number. Out of scope for v1, but the run history should record it from the first build.

**Language rule.** Movement between Strata is a **journey**. Strata are deeper and more complex, never higher or lower. Ascent, descent, climb, up, and down are not used about Strata in rules text, Event text, or UI.

### 1.2 Run Structure
**Stratum 1 is The Whispering Weald**, a living green forest with an unsettling atmosphere. This replaces the earlier build name, The Ashen Weald.

A run is **four Strata**. Each Stratum is a fresh 11x11 Field and exactly 16 pairs (32 spawns), with the Archon in the final pair (2.2, 2.6). Between Strata the player visits an **automatic Tavern**. Winning the run means defeating the final Archon of Stratum 4, **the Apex Predator**, after which the small closing line from the premise appears. Losing means the player's HP reaching 0 at any point in the game with no means of increasing it above 0 in that moment. Unlocks and difficulty levels are planned but **out of scope for v1**.

### 1.3 Two Formats
The software version is the main version. A physical board game version is a design goal, so every major rule must be resolvable with dice, cards, and tokens: spawn locations are determined with dice, random movement reuses the Location dice, statuses and damage are counters on a card. The two versions may differ in details (the software can hide information and automate bookkeeping that the board game cannot, some esoteric software game behaviors might be impractical to replicate physically), but the core rules are shared.

### 1.4 Companions along the journey

Introduce a specific Ally in each Stratum with its artwork and a brief story encounter. The Stratum 1 companion depends on the character; the Druid meets **Sapling**, already part of the starting Grimoire. Do not grant another copy merely for showing the introduction. Future characters' Stratum 1 companions and later Strata's companions remain to be designed.

**Druid opening story:** “At the forest’s edge, a sapling lifts its roots from the earth and falls into step beside you. It pauses when you pause. When you turn toward the darker trees, it shakes the dew from its leaves and follows. You make room in your grimoire. Neither of you has to enter the Weald alone.”

Show Sapling and this story in the normal opening before entering the Field, and in the opening First Clearing tutorial callout so first-time players also meet it. Keep the introduction brief and player-paced. Reuse the existing Sapling art and preserve its current mechanics. Do not reveal the hidden nature of Astrata or preview future companions in this v1 presentation.

**Stratum 2: Machine Elves.** Every character receives this same companion. Stratum 2 introduces new Motes, Eidolons and Archons and focuses on understanding and dealing with Corruptions. Spend Focus to place the Elves directly on a Corruption space, activate them, then wait an extra turn for repair. They have one activation; successful repair removes the Corruption and automatically returns the Elves to discard. Upgrades permit repairing more difficult Corruptions. Protect them: if killed, they follow the normal Ally Destroyed-pile rule for the rest of that battle. The playable v2 rules, including timing, repair tiers and Memory Hole placement, are specified in 10.12. Stratum 2 planning remains in [Stratum 2 planning](stratum_2_planning.md). They remain excluded from Stratum 1 content pools.

## 2. The Field (Travel and Spawning)

### 2.1 Layout
* 11x11 grid, coordinates (0,0) to (10,10), **True Center** at (5,5).
* The player starts at the True Center.
* Spawn locations are rolled with two pairs of dice, one pair per axis, with the 6 sides of each die labeled from 0 to 5.  The numbers in each pair are added to find the coordinate for that axis.  This means the center (5,5) is the most likely landing zone (**Probability Peak**) and the outer ring is nearly empty (only spawns there on Snake Eyes or Double Sixes for one of the pairs).

### 2.2 The Spawn Queue ("What")
A Stratum is **16 spawning rounds of two spawns each: 16 pairs, 32 spawns total**. The player always sees the **next four pairs** in order, or all remaining pairs near the end. **The second slot of pair 8 is the Stratum's only Field Tavern; the second slot of pair 16 is its Archon.** Neither is rolled randomly. The Tavern uses the normal spawn-location rules, stays until visited, and must be reached; its scheduled appearance is not an automatic visit. The automatic between-Strata Tavern (2.6) is separate and remains outside v1.

The other **30 spawns** use two-stage rolls. The design weights are **Mote 40, Eidolon 10, Gold 15, Item 10, Event 20**. With Taverns fixed instead of random, these weights total 95 and are normalized over the random spawns. Later random rolls are therefore approximately **42.11% Mote, 10.53% Eidolon, 15.79% Gold, 10.53% Item, 21.05% Event**.

| First roll: d20 | Category | Second roll: d10 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1–10 | Enemy | 1–8 Mote; 9–10 Eidolon |
| 11–15 | Loot | 1–6 Gold; 7–10 Item |
| 16–19 | Event | No second roll |
| 20 | Reroll the category die | The former random Tavern share is reserved for the scheduled Tavern |

| Four-pair block | Spawn slots | Rule |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 (pairs 1–4) | 8 random rolls | **No Eidolons.** An Eidolon result becomes a Mote. |
| 2 (pairs 5–8) | 7 random rolls **+ the Tavern** | Tavern is the second slot of pair 8. |
| 3 (pairs 9–12) | 8 random rolls | Standard weighted rolls. |
| 4 (pairs 13–16) | 7 random rolls **+ the Archon** | Archon is the second slot of pair 16. |

* Example opening Spawn Queue: `[M + G] [M + V] [G + I] [M + M]`.
* Roll the initial four pairs together, then append one new pair after each pair spawns, keeping four upcoming pairs visible until fewer than four remain. Revealed pairs never reroll. Each member uses its own Location roll, with the first-pair distance rule in 2.3. The scheduled Tavern and Archon become visible when their pairs enter the preview.
* The queue shows only the spawn *type*. Which enemy a Mote or Eidolon turns out to be is hidden until it spawns (2.7). Locations follow 2.3; enemy identities follow 2.7.

### 2.3 Rounds
Each Field round runs in this order:

1. **Spawn.** Both members of the front pair spawn. **First pair only:** independently choose each member’s location uniformly from the **16 spaces exactly two movement steps from the player** (diagonals count as one step): the perimeter of the 5×5 square centered on the player, where max(|ΔX|, |ΔY|) = 2. Both spawns may choose the same space under normal collision rules; each has all 16 possibilities. With the normal central starting position all 16 are on the Field. For a nonstandard edge start, choose only on-board perimeter spaces without clamping them closer. Physical play can number the perimeter clockwise and use two d4s to select 1–16 as 4 × (first roll − 1) + second roll. **Later pairs:** for each member, roll **four Location dice (d6, faces 0-5)**: two summed for X, two summed for Y. Draw enemy identities and group sizes (2.7), place both members, and only then resolve player collisions (2.5), so enemies from the same pair landing on the player fight together. Refill the preview to four pairs where possible.
2. **Player movement.** The player moves up to **2 tiles** (orthogonal or diagonal), or none at all. During this phase they may also open the **inventory** to equip found gear and **swap Armor** (6.2). Items, Spells, and Armor can raise the movement value permanently or for one round; Curses or Hexes can lower it. **Entering any occupied tile ends the move.** Whatever is there resolves at once (Gold is collected, an Item is offered to collect or leave, a Tavern is entered, an Event fires, a Mote/Eidolon is fought), and any movement the player had left this round is **forfeited**. A player with 2 movement who steps onto Gold on the first tile picks it up and stays there.
3. **Enemy movement.** Every enemy on the Field moves according to its movement type (2.7), including any Pack Movement triggered along the way. An enemy stops immediately upon reaching the player's tile and stays there for the rest of the phase; the other enemies finish moving before battle begins. At the **end of the enemy movement phase**, if one or more enemies share the player's tile, one battle begins against all of them, with the enemies taking the opening turn before the player’s first Reveal. During player movement, by contrast, stepping onto an enemy's tile starts the battle at once (2.4).

Then the next round begins with the next Spawn. Newly spawned enemies take their normal movement after the player's movement that round, including round one, subject to the Archon's printed waiting behavior (2.6).

**Battles interrupt the round.** A battle resolves completely before the round continues. If a Bat wanders onto the player during enemy movement, the fight happens at the end of that phase, and only afterwards does the next spawn roll.

**Worked example.** The preview is `[G + G] [M + V] [G + I] [M + M]`. On the first round, both Gold piles appear at independently rolled tiles; the player then moves. Pair 2 shows a Mote and an Event; the Mote's identity and group size are revealed only when it appears. As each pair spawns, one new pair enters the preview. Pair 8 contains a random spawn and the scheduled Tavern; pair 16 contains a random spawn and the Archon at the True Center. Battles interrupt movement as usual, and the Archon still arrives on spawning round 16.

**Armor swap example.** A Restless Undead Fire Wolf moves 4 tiles a round and sits 2 tiles away from the player. The player can only move 2, so the fight is unavoidable. During the movement phase they swap Holy Armor (recover 2 HP on up to two injured player-turn starts per battle) for Water Armor (2 base Guard each enemy round, worth up to3 against Fire before it drains) and walk into the fight prepared.

### 2.4 Resolving a Tile
| Player lands on / is landed on by... | Result |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Mote / Eidolon** | **Battle begins** (Section 3). The tile is cleared on victory. |
| **Gold** | Collected immediately. A Gold pile is worth **10 to 100**, average 50: roll two spare d6 (faces 0-5), sum them, multiply that total by 10, with a minimum of 10. Every round a gold pile sits uncollected it **loses 5**; at 0 it vanishes. |
| **Item** | Draw the top card of the **Item Deck** (2.9) and resolve it: a single Setting, Gem, Armor, Card, Curse, or Hex to collect or leave. |
| **Event** | Narrative event triggers immediately (choices, card offers, risks, enemies, auto-hp gain or loss, random item, etc.). |
| **Tavern** | Player may heal, buy cards, Gems, and Settings, socket Gems, remove a card from the Grimoire, upgrade cards, and pay Gold for gossip (reveal the current Stratum's Archon, hints about upcoming Events). A Tavern stays on the Field until visited, then **closes** and its tile is cleared. |

### 2.5 Spawn Collisions
| Spawn lands on... | Result |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Another enemy** | **Pack join:** the enemies may separate on their next movement phase, but if the player collides with them while overlapped in this way, the player must fight BOTH enemies at the same time. |
| **An enemy and a non-enemy spawn** (either landing on the other) | The enemy guards it (2.7): fight first, then collect or enter what is beneath. |
| **Gold, an Item, a Tavern, or an Event on another of those** | Both stay on the tile. Entering the tile collects or resolves both in one step, in the order they spawned. |
| **The player** | Resolves immediately per 2.4. A Mote or Eidolon landing on the player starts a battle at once. |

### 2.6 Strata and the Archon
The run is **four Strata**. Each Stratum is exactly 16 pairs, totaling 32 spawns (2.2). Between Strata / after an Archon fight the player gets an **automatic Tavern** with full services, then starts the next Stratum at the True Center of a fresh Field. The second spawn of pair 16 in Strata 1 to 3 is always the **Archon**, which spawns at the True Center rather than rolling Location dice. The second spawn of pair 16 in Strata 4 is the final boss, **the Apex Predator**. Defeating the Archon ends the Stratum and opens the next one with a fresh Field. The Apex Predator uses Hunter movement.

**Archon pacing.** The Archon's arrival should give the player a short window, a few Field rounds to move around, collect what is left, and fight smaller enemies, before the Archon fight becomes effectively forced. This window is a design guideline, not a fixed mechanic: each Archon prints its own waiting and pursuit behavior, and the builder tunes it. **Within six Field rounds of the Archon appearing, avoiding the fight should be almost impossible, if not impossible.** Examples: a Hunter Archon that waits four rounds and then hunts; a pursuing Archon that moves 0 tiles on the round it spawns, then 1, 2, 3, 4. A Sentinel Archon must eventually pursue, or have another printed behavior that prevents indefinite evasion. The player may engage sooner, and the usual collision rules apply. Rounds are counted from the round the Archon spawns, which is round one; battle turns do not advance the count. This is the target for v1, which has a single difficulty. Harder difficulties with shorter windows are future work (11.1).

**The Archon Deck.** Each Stratum has three possible Archons. One is drawn face-down at the start of the Stratum and stays hidden. The player can learn which one is coming in two ways:

* **Tavern gossip:** pay Gold or Items to a town gossip, spy, or traveler to occasionally reveal the Archon.
* **Heralds:** some Motes and Eidolons (roughly one in five Motes and one in three Eidolons) carry the **Herald** trait and reveal the Archon's name when defeated.

Knowing the Archon early lets the player shape their Grimoire and Item loadout for it, which is the reason to pay for gossip or pick a fight with a Herald.

### 2.7 Enemy Decks and Field Movement
Each Stratum has a **Mote Deck** and an **Eidolon Deck**. When a Mote or Eidolon spawns, the top card of the matching deck is drawn and that enemy is placed on the rolled tile. The player learns the enemy only at that moment.

**Opening encounter pool.** In the first four spawn pairs (individual spawns 1–8), Motes draw from a separate shuffled, refillable pool of **Bat, Bell Beetle, Veil Moth, and Spark-Wisp**, one copy of each per refill. These encounters are single enemies. Eidolons remain excluded from those opening pairs. From pair 5, draw from the full Mote pool alongside the existing chance of Eidolons; easier Motes remain possible. This restriction applies to Field spawning, not separately authored Event fights. Preserve existing spawned enemies when loading a save; initialize the opening pool lazily for older saves.

**Grouped encounters.** One Field enemy icon can represent several enemies of the same kind. For v1, Bats, Bell Beetles, and Ashlings use one member in spawning rounds 1–4, randomly one or two in rounds 5–10, and randomly two or three from round 11 onward (equal chances within each range). Other enemies remain single unless specified. Determine the count once when the icon spawns and display it on the Field and in inspection. The group moves together as one Field entity; its members become separate battle targets, each with full printed HP, Tells, status, and inherited Restlessness. Victory requires defeating every member and any other enemies sharing the tile. Give one battle reward offer under 6.1; Gold is paid per defeated member, and each extra member slightly improves the card offer (6.1). This grouping is distinct from Pack Movement and does not create extra Field spawn slots.

Every enemy card has a **Field movement type**, used during the enemy movement phase:

| Type | Behavior | Feel |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hunter** | Moves directly onto the player's tile from anywhere on the Field. | One movement phase for the player to react, then the fight is forced; Archons use their printed waiting schedule first (2.6). Reserved for some Eidolons, some Archons, and the Apex Predator. |
| **Stalker N** | Moves N tiles toward the player. N is printed on the enemy card (2 or 3 in average cases). | Fast; must be dealt with within a round or two, otherwise the enemy becomes more and more Restless, which can causes multiple effects making the enemy stronger and/or faster. |
| **Wanderer** | Moves 2 tiles in a rolled direction. | Unpredictable nuisance. |
| **Skittish** | If the player is within 2 tiles, moves 2 tiles directly away. Otherwise moves 1 tile in a rolled direction. | Cannot simply be chased down: get within 3 tiles and wait for it to drift closer, or carry extra movement. Often guards loot or items. |
| **Sentinel**/**Stationary** | Does not move. | A fixed obstacle or a fight the player picks on their own terms. |

**Random movement roll.** A Wanderer rolls **two of the Location dice**, one for X and one for Y. On each die, 0-1 means move negative on that axis, 2-3 means no change, 4-5 means move positive. The two results give one of eight directions, each equally likely, or no movement at all (both dice showing 2-3, a one in nine chance). The Wanderer moves its full distance in that direction, stopping early at the Field edge or upon reaching the player. No extra die is needed in the box; a d8 direction die is an acceptable substitute.

**Skittish movement.** Distance on the Field counts the way the player moves, so a diagonal step is one tile: the distance between two tiles is the larger of their X and Y differences. At its movement, a Skittish enemy checks its distance to the player:

* **Within 2 tiles: it flees.** It moves 2 tiles directly away from the player on each axis, stopping early at the Field edge.
* **3 or more tiles away: it drifts.** It moves 1 tile in a direction rolled exactly like a Wanderer's (the random movement roll above, including the one-in-nine chance of not moving), stopping at the Field edge.

**Catching one.** The player moves before enemies do, so ending a move within 2 tiles of a Skittish enemy only makes it flee. The way to catch it is to end the move **exactly 3 tiles away** and wait. When its drift brings it within 2 tiles, the player reaches it on their next move. A player with **+1 movement** from an item, card, or Armor can reach it from 3 tiles directly. A Skittish enemy that flees into an edge or corner and cannot get further away stays within reach and can be caught on the next move. **Restless tokens add to the flee distance only;** the drift stays 1 tile, so Restlessness makes a Skittish enemy harder to corner but not harder to wait out.

**Movement rules**
* **Enemies may step onto anything.** An enemy can end its move on Gold, an Item, a Tavern, an Event, or another enemy. Nothing blocks entry onto a tile; reaching the player ends that enemy's movement.
* **An enemy standing on a spawn guards it.** If the player steps onto that tile, the fight happens first; when it is won, whatever was underneath is collected or entered immediately (Gold and Items picked up, a Tavern entered, an Event triggered).
* **Enemies stacked on one tile fight together.** A player who steps onto two enemies fights both at once.
* **Stop on the player.** On the first step onto the player's tile, an enemy forfeits its remaining movement and stays there for the rest of the enemy movement phase, including during subsequent Pack Movement. It cannot move through or away from the player. For example, a Bat that reaches the player on the first of two steps stops there instead of taking its second step. The other enemies still finish their movement. Battle begins only when the phase ends, against every enemy then sharing the player's tile.
* **Pack Movement N (group)** is a trait printed as, for example, *Pack Movement 2 (Wolf)*. During the enemy movement phase, whenever any enemy reaches the player's tile, every other enemy with a Pack Movement trait naming a matching group immediately moves N tiles toward the player, whether or not it has already taken its normal movement this phase. Pack Movement can chain: if one brings another packmate onto the player, the rest move again. Enemies that have not yet taken their normal movement still take it afterwards unless they have already reached the player. Enemies already on the player's tile stay there and do not trigger Pack Movement again merely by remaining there.

**Pack Movement worked example.** Wolf (Stalker 2), Fire Wolf (Stalker 2), and Undead Fire Wolf (Stalker 3) all carry *Pack Movement 2 (Wolf)*, meaning it's Pack Movement is triggered by any other enemy containing the word *Wolf*. The Wolf moves first and reaches the player. Fire Wolf and Undead Fire Wolf each immediately move 2 toward the player. If the Fire Wolf now reaches the player too, the Undead Fire Wolf moves another 2. If it has not yet reached the player, it then takes its normal movement of up to 3, stopping immediately if it reaches the player. If that brings it onto the player, upon the end of the enemy movement phase, the battle is fought against all three wolves.

### 2.8 Restlessness (Enemies Left Alone Get Worse)
Avoiding enemies while farming Gold, Items, and Taverns must not be a winning strategy. After pairs 4, 8, and 12 spawn, every enemy still on the Field gains one **Restless token**. Refilling the rolling preview does not add tokens. This retains the four-round cadence despite the doubled spawn count. Each token gives:

* **+1 tile** of Field movement (a Wanderer with two tokens moves 4; Hunters are unaffected).
* **+1 damage** on every attack in battle.
* May have other effects specified on the card

**Map inspection.** Clicking any enemy-occupied tile opens inspection without spending movement, including adjacent reachable enemies. Show spawn pair, elapsed Field rounds since spawn (new arrivals are age 0), actual Restless tokens, group size, HP per member, current effective movement and the first battle rotation with Restless attack bonuses included. Age and Restlessness are distinct: tokens arrive at the four-pair thresholds, not every turn; even an enemy just spawned on a threshold receives that threshold token under the existing rule. Restless does not add HP. Sentinels remain stationary and Hunters/Archons retain their own movement behavior. Missing legacy birth data is labeled unavailable rather than invented. Reachable enemy inspection offers **Move here · start battle**; all enemies sharing the tile appear in the preview. Beside **End Movement**, provide a compact **eight-direction movement pad**, laid out around an empty center in a 3×3 pattern, with arrows for cardinal and diagonal directions. These buttons directly perform the corresponding legal move, including moving onto an enemy to start battle, without opening inspection. Keep ordinary tile movement and enemy-click inspection available. Disable directions outside the Field or otherwise unavailable; honor movement costs, encounter resolution, autosave and animation input locks. Give each arrow an accessible direction label and destination hover information.

In physical play this is a token placed on the enemy card. Restlessness makes Skittish enemies harder to catch and Sentinels a growing threat, so the Field slowly pushes the player toward fighting.

### 2.9 Events and the Item Deck
**Events** work like Slay the Spire events: a card from the Stratum's **Event Deck** is drawn and anything can happen. Examples of the range:

* A forced enemy encounter.
* A trader offering a Spell in exchange for a Bracelet.
* A surge of energy that restores HP
* A forced trudge through a swamp with noxious gas that takes away HP.
* An item, for example a helmet found in the mud (a Crown with a printed bonus: +2 Guard to any activated card with "Shield" in its name).
* A Cursed object with a quest attached, which imparts an automatic negative effect on the player and also guarantees that the next Event that spawns is the side quest that removes that Cursed object (6.6).
* Rarely, a card offer from another class's pool, on of the few ways class card pools ever cross.

Events may appear in more than one Stratum's deck, or might be limited to that particular Stratum 1 through 4.

**The Item Deck** is drawn from whenever an Item spawn is encountered. An Item card is one of: a Setting (which is a Ring, Bracelet, or Necklace), a Gem, an Armor, a Helmet, a Consumable (6.7), a card for the Grimoire, a Cursed Setting/Gem/Armor/Helmet, or a Hex (basically a Curse in the form of a card). Each Item spawn reveals one randomly determined reward with **Collect** and **Leave item** options, including Cursed items and Hexes. Leaving permanently forfeits that pickup, applies none of its effects, and does not restore forfeited movement; any other contents of the tile still resolve. Choices between items belong to Events with an explicit story reason for the offer. An Event may require accepting an item as part of its outcome or explicitly offer a way to decline it; the Event text determines which.

## 3. Battle (The Mind Grid)

### 3.1 Setup
**Initiative from the Field.** When an enemy reaches the player during enemy movement (including Pack Movement), finish all Field enemy movement, then let all enemies in that encounter perform their first normal cycle move before the player’s first Reveal. A utility first move remains a utility move. Apply normal battle-start effects first, including Opening Rite and recurring Curses; equipped defenses are available against the opening attack. After the enemy turn, begin the first player turn normally. Preserve an unresolved opening defense across save/resume without repeating attacks. Walking onto an enemy, an enemy spawning on the player, and Event encounters retain player-first initiative.

When a battle begins the player's play space consists of three things: their **Grimoire** (deck), the empty **7x6 Mind Grid**, and the enemy or enemies with their **Tells** (next telegraphed action, though in some cases the next action might be hidden).

Battle stats always begin using the player's **starting values** at the start of every fight:

| Economy | Action | Resource Term | Default start | What it governs |
| :--- | :---| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sight | See | **Insight** | 4 | Number of cards revealed from the Grimoire each turn to choose from for placement|
| Placement | Place | **Focus** | 1 | Total Focus cost of cards that can be placed on the grid this turn |
| Activation | Activate | **Channel** | 2 | Total Channel available for activations this turn; printed costs and per-card activation limits still apply |

Socketed Gems (6.2) and certain cards raise starting values for the entire run, while equipped. A late-run player might start at Insight 7, Focus 4, Channel 3.

**Temporary enemy resource reductions.** Non-damaging Mote moves may reduce the next player turn’s resources: Bat **Screech −2 Insight**, Bell Beetle **Dulling Chime −1 Focus**, Veil Moth **Dazzle −1 Channel**. These are single-turn penalties, stack additively across enemies, and clear after that turn’s resource allocation. Insight retains a minimum of 1; Focus and Channel can reach 0 but never go negative. Apply penalties to the player’s current modified resource baseline without permanently changing equipment or card bonuses. Zero-Focus cards remain placeable at 0 Focus. Clearly include the amount and **next turn only** in the enemy Tell.

**Resource counters show remaining resources.** During Reveal, Insight counts down as cards turn face up and is 0 when Reveal finishes (also if too few cards remain). Focus and Channel decrease as spent; show depleted counters clearly. Thus a player who uses all resources reaches 0 / 0 / 0 before pressing the Activation → Enemy arrow. Do not fake expenditure: any unspent Focus or Channel stays visible until the turn ends. Pressing the arrow immediately refills the counters to the next turn's current values (normally 4 / 1 / 2, including equipment, battle-long and queued next-turn modifiers) while enemies resolve. Refilling does not reopen placement or activation. Enemy effects can still change the upcoming values; reconcile them before the next Reveal, which spends Insight again. Unused resources do not carry over.

Starting HP depends on class (4.1) and averages about 60. HP does **not** reset between battles. It recovers at Taverns, through Holy Armor, and through a few cards, consumable items, or lucky events.

### 3.2 Turn Structure
**Player turn**
1. **Reveal.** Reveal/Draw cards face up from the Grimoire. **The Grimoire has no order:** revealed cards are drawn at random from what remains, so inspecting the Grimoire and discard pile is always allowed and reveals nothing about what comes next. (In the physical game the Grimoire is a shuffled face-down stack and cannot be inspected)
2. **Place.** Choose revealed cards and put them on empty grid slots, up to total *Focus*. Unchosen revealed cards go to the discard pile.  Some cards have a Focus cost more than 1, or might have a free Focus cost (0).
3. **Activate.** Activate placed cards, paying their printed Channel costs. **Each card may activate only once per turn by default**, even if its total activation limit has uses remaining. A card with **Blink** explicitly printed in its text may activate more than once that turn, paying its activation cost for every use and still respecting its total activation limit. Effects resolve immediately, including effects that raise Insight, Focus, or Channel for the next turn or permanently, doing damage to one or more enemies, defending damage from enemies on their next attack, or other effects.
4. **Recall (optional, any time during Place).** Return a card from the grid to the discard pile by paying its **Recall cost** in Focus (1 for most cards). Recalled cards rejoin the Grimoire at the next reshuffle, after the remaining cards have been drawn. Recall restores activation allowance and resets Charges; Wards return to their initial value on subsequent placement, not while in discard. **Allies cannot be Recalled unless their card explicitly permits it.**
5. **End turn.** The player ends the turn explicitly. Unused Focus and Channel are lost unless a card or item says otherwise.

**Phases are one-way.** Effects never reopen a completed phase. Resource gains apply this turn or next turn according to when they can be used: Insight gained during activation applies to the next reveal, Focus gained during activation to the next placement phase, and Channel gained during activation can be spent that turn. Card text must state the timing; battle-long increases retain their stated duration.

**Targeting.** A card that deals damage hits **one enemy of the player's choice** unless it says otherwise. The exceptions are cards that say **all enemies** and cards that hit **random enemies**. There are no cards that let the player divide one card's damage among chosen enemies.

**Enemy turn**
Each enemy performs the action shown by its Tell, then reveals its next Tell. Enemy behavior is a fixed, recognizable rotation with little or no randomness, so the player can plan around it. Each attack's **element is printed on the Tell**; most match the enemy's own element or are Arcane, but not all.  The behavior cycles do scale, (more damage done based on the number of times the enemy has gone through its cycle), so prolonging the battle ultimately makes it more difficult.

**Boss Purify.** After the last normal move of each full cycle, an Archon checks for any remaining Burn, Poison or Corrode (after that move's elemental-immunity changes). If afflicted, its next Tell is **Purify**: spend one whole enemy move removing all three statuses, including any added during the intervening player turn. Then resume the first normal move on the following turn. If clean at the cycle boundary, skip Purify; statuses added afterward wait for the next boundary. Once telegraphed, Purify remains committed even if the original status expires. Existing status ticks occur before the boss's move, so they can still kill it before Purify; death effects such as Final Note still resolve. Purify does not heal HP, remove Guard or change element, and does not advance the normal cycle or its damage scaling. Ordinary every-enemy-turn passives still apply. This rule applies to bosses, not Motes, Eidolons or Mini-Voids. Save its pending state and expose the Tell to the player and AI.

**Grid targeting is deterministic.** An enemy effect that touches the Mind Grid never picks a random card. It names one of these rules, and ties are broken by **reading order** (top row left to right, then the next row down):

* **Newest / Oldest:** the most or least recently placed card.
* **Most Connected:** the card with the most adjacent cards.
* **Tallest:** the slot holding the most cards.
* **Fullest Column / Fullest Row:** the line holding the most cards.
* **Weakest / Strongest Ally:** by current HP.

**Act 1 boss targeting previews.** The player receives one full player turn (Reveal, Placement and Activation) before the upcoming destructive move; this is not an extra turn or an extra placement allowance. When the boss's next Tell is destructive, visibly outline the threatened spaces and mark the cards that would be destroyed. Cinder Hart's **Wildfire** targets the Fullest Row; Void-Colossus's **Collapse** targets the Fullest Column; Glass Choir's **Shatter Hymn** targets the two most valuable complete stacks on distinct spaces. Rank stacks by **combined damage/defense potential** across all cards, including covered cards, then printed Focus cost, remaining activation allowance and reading order. For each card, take the largest of its potential direct damage from one release, current Ward value, Ward gain from one activation, Shield output from one activation, currently stored Shield Guard, or current Ally HP; sum these card values for the stack. Include current intrinsic damage bonuses, independently usable pile cards, multi-element hits and all-enemy damage across living enemies, but no equipment bonuses, elemental matchups or future status ticks; random damage uses its average. Spent cards have no future activation output, but retained Ward/Shield values and Ally HP still count. Charged cards such as Kiln and Patient Seed count their eventual release if their remaining allowance can reach the threshold; do not multiply by lifetime uses. This makes dangerous attacks and substantial defenses primary targets rather than expensive utility cards. A card marked unplaceable has no printed placement-cost value; do not treat the implementation's sentinel as a cost. Remaining allowance includes current allowance bonuses, regardless of whether the card has already activated this turn. Select both targets before destroying either; a stack of two or more cards counts as one target and is destroyed completely. If fewer than two stacks exist, destroy all that exist. Preview both targets and update after placement, Recall or activation; this is a live forecast, not a locked selection. Row ties choose the topmost row and column ties the leftmost column. No random tie-breaking. An empty grid threatens no cards.

These are **live targeting previews**, not committed targets: update immediately after placement, stacking, Recall, Shift, destruction or any other relevant board change. Resolve against the board when the enemy actually acts, using the exact same targeting function as the preview. Multiple enemies may change the board before a later enemy acts; previews describe the current board and are recalculated as resolution proceeds. Show the move name and number of threatened cards on the enemy, with hover help explaining the targeting rule and tie-breaker. Keep card art, activation controls and status symbols readable; distinguish danger with both outlines and a warning symbol, not color alone. Do not dim the entire battlefield or add an approval click. Provide the same visible target information to AI observations without revealing hidden state or consuming RNG. Normal turns have no destruction markings.

Later mechanics may instead commit to marked locations when responding to that fixed space is their intended challenge. Clearly distinguish committed marks from live previews; never silently retarget a mark presented as fixed. One-turn warning and clear consequences are the default for major future grid disruptions, including Corruption placement and Shear; Mind Mine additionally carries its own visible fuse.

When the Grimoire is empty the discard pile is shuffled to form a new Grimoire.

### 3.3 Card Anatomy
**Adjacent** means the four orthogonal neighbors (up, down, left, right) unless a card says it also counts diagonals.

**Rounding.** Two rules, applied everywhere:
* **Damage dealt or absorbed rounds up.** Half of 5 is 3. One and a half times 5 is 8.
* **Resource values round down with a floor of 1.** Insight, Focus, Channel, and Field movement halved: 5 becomes 2, 4 becomes 2, 3 becomes 1, 2 becomes 1, 1 stays 1. A card must say so explicitly to take a resource down to 0.

Every card has:

* **Type:** **Spell** (acts when activated), **Ally** (has HP, can intercept), **Ward** (holds a ward value), or **Object** (utility, economy, or passive effect).
* **Element:** Fire, Earth, Wind, Water, Chaos, Light, or Arcane (none).
* **Focus cost:** 1 for most cards; 0 for a few; 2 or 3 or more for stronger cards. Objects whose only payoff is temporary extra Insight or Focus on the next turn should cost **0 Focus to place**; their activation cost, grid slot, and activation allowance are sufficient costs. In v1 this applies to **Clear Mind** (+2 Insight next turn), **Tide Memory** (+1 Insight next turn per connected orthogonally adjacent occupied space, up to +4), and **Rain Lantern** (+2 Focus next turn). This does not make permanent resource increases or effects with additional benefits free by default.
* **Activation limit:** how many times it can be activated in total before it becomes **Spent** (informally, a dud). This is separate from the default once-per-turn restriction: an activation limit of 3 allows one activation on each of three turns, not three activations on one turn. An unlimited total limit also does not grant repeat activations within a turn. Per-turn availability refreshes on the next player turn; the total allowance does not. A Spent card cannot activate and occupies its slot until Recalled or covered. Its Stack rules still apply, and a Ward with remaining ward value still absorbs damage (3.3.3).
* **Activation cost:** Channel spent per activation. Default 1.
* **Blink (optional status):** explicitly printed in the card text. The card may activate multiple times in one turn, paying its printed activation cost each time. Blink does not replenish or bypass its total activation limit, other activation conditions, or resource costs. Track per-turn activation separately for each card instance, including covered cards activated by Pile or Fusion. Moving or covering a card does not refresh its per-turn availability. Building Charge counts as an activation. At the next player turn, the per-turn opportunity refreshes but spent total allowance does not.
* **Recall cost:** Focus needed to return the card from the grid to the discard pile during placement phase. Default 1. Cheap-to-place cards might have a high Recall cost, trading early reward for later clutter. A few cards **cannot be Recalled** at all and stay on the grid, Spent, for the rest of the battle.
* **Effect:** damage, healing, status, or an economy boost (extra Insight, Focus, or Channel this turn or next, as appropriate to the phase order) that occurs when the card is activated during the Activation phase.
* **HP-healing cards:** cards whose activation heals the player or an Ally are scarce, rare cards with exactly one activation per battle. Resolve the full effect, then immediately move that card to Destroyed, even if no HP was missing. They cannot Recall or gain extra uses (including from Keystone); triggered activations beneath a stack also consume the healer, leaving other cards intact. They return next battle under the usual Destroyed-pile rule. Ally growth (including Sapling's post-attack growth), equipment healing, and Event/Tavern recovery are separate effects. Equipment must not allow unlimited healing within a battle: use consumable or bounded triggers, normally once or twice. Holy Armor and Necklace of Dew each have two healing triggers per battle, consumed only on player-turn starts while injured; full HP spends none. Track uses per owned item, preserve them across saves, and reset only for a new battle. Healing resolves before ongoing status damage. Show remaining healing triggers on equipped items.
* **On place (optional):** a placement bonus that triggers the moment the card is placed, before any activation. Costs no Channel.  This effect is Rare.
* **Charge (optional):** Charge N means N stored charges are required before a separate release activation. While below N, an activation adds 1 Charge unless the card specifies a bonus; filling the counter never releases the effect on the same activation. Charges cap at N and excess is discarded. Once full, the next activation releases the effect and resets Charge to 0. Both charging and release spend the printed Channel cost and one activation allowance; the usual once-per-turn rule applies unless Blink is printed. Charges persist across turns and reset on Recall. Charging needs no enemy target; targeted releases select one, while all-enemy/random releases remain targetless. Show current/required Charge (including 0/N), separately from remaining activations. A fully charged card with no activations left is Spent and cannot release. Damage previews, AI decisions and boss card-value targeting must include the separate release activation and any currently available charging bonus.
* **Card Rarity:** common, rare, or legendary.
* **Upgrade (optional):** a minority of cards have one or more upgraded forms that make them stronger. **Most cards have no upgrade.** Upgrading happens at Taverns and in some Events. **The requirement is printed on the card and varies by card**, always with flavor text explaining why: pay Gold to a skilled expert met in an Event or Tavern, destroy another card of equal rarity, destroy two cards of lesser rarity, accept one or two deck-clogging cards in the Grimoire, pay a large sum of Gold, or suffer a one-time HP loss. Some upgrades also have conditions, such as wearing three Fire-imbued pieces of equipment (6.2). A sorcerer NPC who charges Gold gives Sorcerer players a noticeable but small discount, on the order of 150 instead of 170.
* **Activation Condition (optional):** a placement rule that gates activation. Examples:
    * *Isolated:* cannot be activated while adjacent to any other card.
    * *Bonded (Fire):* cannot be activated unless adjacent to a Fire card.
    * *Cornerstone:* can only be activated from a corner slot.
* **Pattern scaling (optional):** the effect grows with a shape on the grid, such as +2 damage per Fire card in the same row, or double effect when four cards form a 2x2 block.
* **Stack (optional):** the card may be placed onto an occupied slot. **Stack is a property of the card, and the card's own text says what it may be placed on and what happens to the card beneath.** Shared defaults, which a card may override:
    * Only the top card of a slot can be activated. Cards beneath are inert unless the stacking card specifies what remains functional. This does not change when the top card becomes Spent unless its text says otherwise. Covered Wards do not absorb damage unless the top card permits it.
    * A slot counts as one card for adjacency and pattern purposes.
    * An enemy effect that destroys a slot destroys everything in it.
    * Recalling a slot means paying the Recall cost of every card in it, unless the top card says otherwise.

  **Levels.** The bottom card of a slot is **level 1**, the card above it level 2, and so on. A lone card is level 1. Some cards refer to levels structurally, such as doubling every card that sits at their own level.

  **Towers.** A card subtype. All Wards are Towers, and some Objects are Towers. Some cards may only be Stacked on a Tower.  Towers can often Stack on each other.

  Stack flavors seen so far:
    * **Pile** (Plasma Ball): stacks only on itself; one Channel fires every ball with an available activation. Each firing ball spends one of its own uses and still obeys the once-per-turn rule. Adding a new ball never refreshes covered balls. Recall moves the whole pile together at 1 Focus per ball before explicit Recall discounts.
    * **Supersede** (Palimpsest): stacks on anything; the card beneath is stuck and inert for this battle. Cheap way to reclaim a slot clogged by a Spent card with a high Recall cost.
    * **Recall-on-place** (Undertow): stacks on anything; the card beneath is Recalled to the discard pile for free. A card that specifically cannot be Recalled simply stays beneath as a dud, as if Undertow had the Supersede effect instead.
    * **Fusion** (Heat on any Water or Earth Spell): stacks only on cards matching a printed condition and, when activated, also fires the card beneath and adds to its effect.
    * **Structural** (Magnifying Glass Tower): stacks only on a Tower and cares about which level it ends up at.  The Magnifying Glass Tower must be at least Level 2 and increases the power of all cards that sit at its same level.

### 3.3.1 Statuses
**On enemies and the player**

**Battle only.** Burn, Poison, and Corrode exist only during battle. All of them are removed when a battle ends, on the player, on enemies, and on Allies. A Cursed object or Hex may apply one of them automatically; its status is still removed when the battle ends, and the Curse or Hex applies it again at the start of the next battle.

* **Burn N:** damage that fades. The target takes N damage at the start of its turn, then N drops by 1, until it reaches 0. Burn 2 deals 2 then 1. Burn 6 deals 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 for 21 total. Tracked as a counter.
* **Poison N:** continuous damage. The target takes N damage at the start of every one of its turns until it is healed. Poison 2 deals 2 each turn until, for example, a card like Soothe cures it. Tracked as a counter.
* **Corrode N:** damage that builds. The target takes N damage at the start of its turn, then N rises by 1, until something stops it. Corrode is usually applied as **Corrode 1**: 1 damage, then 2, then 3, then 4, a slow build that is inevitably destructive. The player's cards may Corrode enemies. **Enemy Corrode can only target the player or an Ally card**, since it needs HP to eat; on an Ally it ticks at the start of the player's turn. Tracked as a counter.

**Enemy elemental immunities.** Water enemies cannot suffer Burn; Fire enemies cannot suffer Poison; Chaos enemies cannot suffer Corrode. These immunities apply to every source, including Fusion and area effects, without preventing a card's separate direct damage or other effects. Immunity follows the enemy's current element: changing into an immune element immediately clears that status, and changing away does not restore it. Thus Chaotic Glare can clear the newly immune status; each Mini-Void uses its own birth element. These elemental immunities do not extend to the player or Allies. Show the current immunity in enemy details and beside enemy health, explain it in keyword tooltips, and provide feedback when an application is prevented.

**Cinder Hart exception.** Cinder Hart is immune to both **Burn and Poison** from every source. This is a boss-specific immunity; ordinary Fire enemies can still suffer Burn. Corrode applies and increases normally, and the existing cycle-boundary Purify can remove it. Clear preexisting Burn/Poison on Hart when loading older saves or checkpoints, and suppress already-queued ticks of those statuses. Show both immunities in its description and battle display, and account for both in AI decisions.

**Stacking and defense.** Repeated applications add status values: Burn 3 applied to Burn 2 becomes Burn 5; Poison and Corrode also add values, then follow their own constant or increasing behavior. On the player, ongoing Burn, Poison, and Corrode damage goes directly to HP: it bypasses Wards, Shields, Allies, Bracelet Guard, and Armor or other generic attack reduction. Attack defenses apply only to enemy attacks, not damage from effects already affecting the player. An item must explicitly name a status to prevent or reduce that status; healing and status-clearing effects still work. An explicitly printed per-hit reduction of2 subtracts2 damage (minimum0), not two whole attacks. Numeric Armor instead spends its finite Guard pool under3.3.3; it does not reduce every hit independently.

**On placed cards** (inflicted by enemies, tracked as a token on the card, cleared when the card leaves the grid)
* **Lock:** the card cannot be Shifted or Recalled. It can still be activated and can still be covered by a Stack card.
* **Freeze:** the card cannot be activated
* **Sever:** the card counts as having no adjacent cards, and no other card counts it as adjacent. It receives no adjacency or pattern bonuses and contributes to none. A side effect: a Severed card satisfies the Isolated condition, so a card that wants to be alone is happy to be Severed.
* **Siphon:** the card loses 1 activation limit.

### 3.3.2 Elements
Four elements sit in a cycle. **Chaos** and **Light** sit in their own cycle (both are strong against each other). **Arcane** means no element at all.

**Fire -> Earth -> Wind -> Water -> Fire**

**Remember the cycle:** Fire burns Earth. Earth blocks Wind. Wind dries Water. Water douses Fire. **Chaos swallows Light; Light overwhelms Chaos.** These verbs describe advantage for attacks, defensive elemental attunements and equipment alike; Chaos and Light are mutually strong against each other.

* **Forward:** an element deals **+50% damage** (round up) to the element it points at. Fire hits Earth for 1.5x: 5 becomes 8.
* **Backward:** an element deals **-50% damage** (round up) to the element that points at it. Fire hits Water for 0.5x: 5 becomes 3.
* Same element, or an unrelated pair (Fire against Wind), is normal damage.
* **Arcane** damage takes no element math in either direction.
* **Chaos** deals **+50% damage** (round up) to **Light**
* **Light** deals **+50% damage** (round up) to **Chaos**
* **Resistances are enemy traits, not part of the cycle.** *Resist Fire* means half damage from Fire, printed on the enemy card. A Fire enemy can resist Fire, which is exactly what makes the Undead Fire Wolf (5.2) painful for a Fire deck.

**Attune.** Some Arcane cards carry the **Attune** keyword. On each activation, the player chooses an element from the adjacent cards, or chooses **Unattune** to use Arcane even when elemental neighbors are available, **unless the card says otherwise** (for example, "Attunes to every adjacent element at once"). A Fire card beside **Blast** offers a **Fire Blast**; beside **Shield**, a **Fire Shield**. Changing adjacent cards changes the choices for the next activation, not the element of an already resolved activation. Each activation may choose anew, including within the same turn when Blink permits another activation. For a Shield, the player picks the attunement at the moment of each activation, and the Guard that activation adds keeps that element as its own portion. A Shield holding portions of different elements works like several Shields: when a hit reaches it, the player chooses which portion takes it, and the convert-back rule applies to that portion only.

**Attunement relays.** After a card with Attune commits an activation, it offers that chosen element to adjacent Attune cards until the next player turn begins. This permits chains such as Earth card → activated Earth Shield → activated Earth Blast → another Shield. Activation order matters, and each card pays its normal Channel cost and allowance. Selecting or previewing an element does not establish or change a relay. Borrowing an element never changes its supplier. A committed relay persists even if the card has spent its activation allowance, its Shield Guard has depleted, or its original elemental source is later removed. Sever and covering prevent connections as usual; only exposed, connected cards can supply or receive a relay. Recall/removal removes the source; a fresh placement has no relay. All temporary relays expire together before the next player turn's actions, so a pair of Arcane cards cannot sustain an element indefinitely by borrowing from each other. A Blink card's latest committed activation determines its offered element, including Arcane if Unattuned; existing Shield portions retain their own elements. Transmute overrides the relay with its fixed element and continues supplying that element in later rounds without activation. Relays affect Attune choices only, not the card's underlying element for other printed-element conditions or bonuses. Show the current relay element and source connections; preserve active relays in exact-state saves.

**Transmute.** A transmuted card uses the chosen element for the rest of its placement, overriding Attune even if adjacent cards offer another element. This includes explicitly transmuting to Arcane. Another Transmute can change it again; leaving the grid and being placed anew restores its printed element and normal Attune behavior. Existing Shield portions retain the elements they had when created. Show the current element on the placed card and in its full details; a Water-transmuted Blast must visibly read as Water Blast.

**Elemental defense.** Use the same cycle and multipliers as offense for Attuned Shield Guard, Gem-imbued Bracelet Guard and elemental Armor protection: **+50% against the element the defense beats; -50% against the element that beats the defense**, rounding the effective protection up. Same-element, unrelated and Arcane matchups are neutral. Earth protection is strongest against Wind and weakest against Fire; Fire protection is strongest against Earth and weakest against Water. Chaos and Light each gain +50% Guard against the other, consistent with their mutual offensive advantage. Apply this multiplier to the defensive value, never to the player's entire incoming hit: weak Armor provides less protection but never increases damage above wearing no Armor. Allies continue resolving incoming elemental damage against their own element (including mutual Chaos/Light vulnerability); Wards remain neutral. Explicit printed enemy Resist traits remain separate.


**Four elements adjacent.** A rare condition. Cards that reward it simply say so: "X occurs if each of the four elements is adjacent to this card." With orthogonal adjacency that uses all four neighbors, so such a card is rarely placed on an edge.

**There is no starting element.** Every element's cards are in every class card pool. Focusing on one element is a build choice that buys synergy and takes on a risk, so any focused deck needs an answer to enemies that resist that focus or sit backward on the cycle from its element. Arcane damage such as an un-Attuned Blast is the fallback in most cases.  Exception:  The Elemental Wisp enemy takes half damage from Arcane attacks and double damage from attacks imbued with any of the elements.

In physical play the cycle is printed on the player board as a small ring.

### 3.3.3 Defense: Armor, Shields, Wards, and Allies
The player has **no defensive stat of their own**. Damage is avoided in five ways, each with a real cost:

1. **Armor and Bracelets** (6.2), chosen on the Field before the fight. Armor protection and Bracelet Guard need no activation or Channel and are optional, selected to protect the player.
2. **Wards**, cards that hold persistent **ward value** and absorb damage when chosen as an eligible defender.
3. **Shields**, cards that **absorb** damage for one turn only, with the player choosing which Shield takes each hit. They must be activated to create Guard, within their printed activation allowance. Bracelets provide ready Guard without activation.
4. **Allies**, cards with HP that step in front of attacks by the player's choice.
5. **Denial:** killing the enemy first, or wasting its turn (Burn, Flicker-style effects, Lock-breaking).

**Positional resolution for an attack on the player**
1. Columns are numbered 1–7 from the player on the left toward the enemies on the right. A fresh hit begins beyond column 7. **Wards, Shields, and Allies have no type-based priority:** all eligible defenders are offered together.
2. Choose a defending card. It absorbs all it can. Any remaining attack now sits in that card's column and may next hit a card **in the same column or any column closer to the player**, regardless of row. It can never move back toward the enemy. Choosing column 5 excludes columns 6 and 7 for this hit; columns 1–5 remain eligible. Covered-card permissions, remaining Ward/Guard/HP, and special attack bypasses still apply.
3. Armor and Bracelets/item Guard occupy the final position beside the player and may be chosen in either order. They may be chosen directly, passing all grid defenses; once equipment is used, no grid card can defend against that hit. Each Bracelet keeps its own Guard, refills at the start of every enemy turn, requires no Channel, and uses elemental Guard conversion. Numeric Armor holds a Guard pool that drains across all hits and enemies in the round, just like Bracelet Guard. Refill it to its printed value at the start of each enemy turn, not before each hit. Use the same elemental conversion and leftover-Guard rounding as Bracelets; preserve any remainder and spend no Channel. Mirror Armor reflects only the chosen hit, once per battle. Choose another eligible item if damage remains.
4. At any ordinary defense choice, **click the Druid to take the hit**. This skips all remaining cards, item Guard and unused Armor protection, preserving their availability for later hits. Remaining damage goes directly to HP. If no eligible defense remains, proceed automatically to HP. Burn, Poison, and Corrode bypass attack defenses entirely (3.3.1).
5. Every separate hit starts a fresh route, including each hit of a multi-hit move. The chosen route persists across decisions and exact-state saves. Pierce bypasses all grid cards; Cull bypasses Wards and Shields. Taunt and explicitly targeted attacks remain exceptions as described below; neither can send damage backward.

**Direct defense controls.** Pulse every eligible defending card, equipment item, and the Druid portrait together while a defense choice is pending. Use a gentle outline/glow without fading the art; reduced motion uses steady highlights. Clicking the Druid accepts the remaining hit and saves unused defenses; no separate Take hit button appears below the battlefield. Outside a defense choice, the portrait opens character details as usual. Click the card itself or its Defend button to absorb the hit; do not require selecting its name from a list below the battlefield. If the clicked space has multiple eligible Shield portions or explicitly usable covered Wards, offer those choices beside that card. Keep a separate View control for details. Remove eligibility highlights from passed columns and explain the restriction on hover. Keep the incoming damage display and attack details; the glowing attack orb resumes from the previous defender.

**Ward rules**
* A Ward is an advanced defensive spell.
* A Ward card starts with **0 ward value** on placement and cannot absorb damage until activated. Activations build ward value, which persists through the battle. This applies to Ward-type cards, including Living Lattice; placement grants no free absorption. Recall and subsequent placement reset the value to 0.
* Wards absorb only when the player chooses an eligible Ward. There is no FIFO priority. The chosen Ward loses up to the remaining base damage, and overflow can pass to any eligible defender in the same column or closer to the player. Unchosen Ward value remains for later hits.
* A Ward with no activations remaining still absorbs damage until its ward value is depleted; track activation allowance and ward value separately.
* A Ward whose earned ward value is depleted to 0 by damage becomes **Spent**. A newly placed Ward at 0 is not Spent and can still activate. It stays on the grid clogging its slot until Recalled or covered, unlike an Ally, which leaves the grid when destroyed.
* Wards are Arcane and take no element math. Pierce attacks ignore Wards, Shields, and Allies. Cull attacks ignore Wards and Shields. Bracelets and Armor still apply to both unless the attack says it ignores equipment.

**Shield rules**
* A Shield is a basic defensive spell that is in every class's starting deck.
* An unupgraded, unattuned Shield gains **4 Guard, plus 1 per adjacent exposed Shield,** each time it is activated, and the Guard lasts only until the end of the coming enemy turn. Its default total activation allowance is **2**, like Blast. Without Blink it activates only once per turn, at 1 Channel per activation. After both uses it is Spent; Recall resets its allowance when replayed. A Shield therefore competes with damage cards for Channel every turn. If a Shield explicitly has Blink, activating it twice in one turn costs 2 Channel and adds 8 Guard before adjacency bonuses.
* **Each Shield keeps its own Guard.** Guard never pools across Shields. The player may choose an eligible Shield portion at any defense decision; it absorbs as much as its current Guard permits. Overflow can pass to any eligible defender in the same column or closer to the player. Unused Guard remains for later attacks in that enemy turn.
* **Shield use is optional.** The player may choose another eligible card, equipment, or Take hit while a Shield still has Guard.
* Shield carries **Attune**, chosen at the moment of each activation; each activation's Guard keeps its own element (3.3.2). Attuned Guard gains +50% against the element it beats, loses 50% against its weakness, and is neutral against the same element or Arcane. Round effective Guard up, then convert any leftover back at the same rate, rounded down. Example: a Fire Shield with 5 Guard faces an Earth attack of 4. Its Guard counts as 8, stops all 4, and the remaining4 converts back to2 Guard. Against Fire4 it simply retains1 Guard. Against Water8, its5 Guard counts as3, stops3 and passes5 onward, leaving0 Guard.
* Shields can be Locked, Severed, Siphoned, covered, and Recalled like any card. Sever removes adjacent-element choices for subsequent activations; it does not change already resolved Guard.

**Ally rules**
* An Ally is a card type with printed **HP** and an optional element and traits. It is placed, Locked, Severed, Siphoned, and covered by Stack cards like any other card. **An Ally cannot be Recalled unless its card explicitly permits it**; normally it dies and goes to the Destroyed pile until the next battle. A covered Ally cannot intercept unless the covering card specifies otherwise.
* The player may choose any eligible Ally to intercept, regardless of available Wards or Shields. The Ally takes the remaining damage with its elemental matchup applied. It can intercept only once per hit; every separate hit offers a fresh choice.
* **Intercepts are all-or-nothing.** An Ally takes the whole remaining attack or none of it. The player can never send part of a Bite to the Ally and the rest to themselves. If the attack exceeds the Ally's HP, the Ally is destroyed and the remainder goes to another eligible defender in the same column or closer to the player, or through to the player, **unless the card says otherwise**. Some Allies are printed to swallow the whole attack even when it kills them (see Energy Guardian, 5.1).
* **Elemental spillover: keep base damage separate from defender-specific bonus damage.** The attack retains its original element throughout interception. A weakness bonus is not ordinary damage and must not be folded into the base remainder passed to another defender. Against a weak Ally, its HP absorbs the weakness bonus first, then base damage. If the next defender does not share that weakness, discard any unabsorbed weakness bonus before resolving that defender's own elemental interaction. Example: 6 Fire damage reaches a 2-HP Earth Ally. It faces 6 base + 3 weakness damage, absorbs 2 of the bonus, and dies. The remaining 1 bonus is discarded on reaching a player with no elemental weakness: 6 damage proceeds to the player's Bracelets/Armor, not 7. Existing equipment defenses still apply. Consecutive Allies with the same weakness share the unspent bonus without regenerating it: 6 Fire → 2-HP Earth → 2-HP Earth leaves 5 base damage for a neutral player. On a change of elemental matchup, discard the old bonus and apply the new defender's matchup to the remaining base: a newly weak defender receives a new bonus of ceil(base / 2); a neutral defender receives base damage. For a resistant Ally, damage received is ceil(base / 2). If it dies, pass on min(incoming base, 2 × max(0, received damage − Ally HP)) as base damage; its resistance does not permanently reduce the attack for later defenders. Example: 6 Fire → 2-HP Earth → 2-HP Water leaves 2 base damage for the player. Odd example: 5 Fire → 2-HP Water deals 3 to Water and passes 2 base onward. If an Ally survives or swallows the hit, nothing continues. Reset this tracking for every separate hit; preserve it while choosing defenders and across exact-state saves. Damage previews must show base and any remaining weakness bonus separately and calculate each available defender's actual outcome. The player has no inherent element in v1; when an Ally’s overflow reaches a Ward, Shield, equipment, or player, discard the Ally weakness bonus and resolve that defense normally. The old behavior of repeatedly multiplying a single spillover total is incorrect.
* An Ally at 0 HP is destroyed and goes to the Destroyed pile (not reshuffled into the deck, unlike discards, and not returned to the Grimoire until the end of battle).
* **Pierce** attacks skip Wards, Shields, and Allies. Bracelets and Armor still apply unless the attack says otherwise, for example "10 damage. Pierce. Ignores Armor and other equipped items." **Cull** attacks bypass Wards and Shields but may still be intercepted by Allies.
* Allies may have activations of their own (attack, heal an adjacent card, taunt), which spend Channel like any card.
* **Taunt.** Taunt is a single status held by **exactly one Ally or by none**. When an Ally taunts, it takes the status away from whichever Ally held it before. While an Ally holds Taunt, **every hit goes to it first, automatically**, before any Ward, Shield, other Ally, or equipment: the player gets no defense choice while it holds Taunt. Only attacks that bypass Allies, such as Pierce, skip it. The hit moves to the taunting Ally's column, so defenders farther toward the enemy are excluded for the rest of that hit. When the taunting Ally is destroyed, its overflow resumes normal positional choices from its column, and the Taunt status is gone. **It does not pass back** to an Ally that taunted earlier: no Ally holds Taunt until another Ally taunts. **Taunt expires at the end of the coming enemy phase**, or earlier if its holder is destroyed or another Ally taunts. To keep it, the Ally must taunt again on a later turn. Attacks explicitly targeting the weakest Ally choose the weakest reachable Ally, breaking ties in reading order.

**General principle: damage decisions are atomic.** Choose the defender, never a partial amount to absorb. The chosen Ward, Shield, Ally, or item absorbs all it can; overflow then continues through the remaining positional choices. A defender that swallows the entire hit ends it even if destroyed.

The difference in feel: a Ward holds persistent absorption, a Shield needs activation to create temporary Guard, and an Ally risks its HP. Their positions determine the available order. In physical play, use an attack token to track its current column; each defender retains its own counters.

Armor protects the player and not Shields, Wards, or Allies, so a Water Armor player facing the Undead Fire Wolf may prefer to take some hits personally and keep their Allies alive for the Sever turn.

### 3.4 The Economies to Balance
* **Placement economy:** Focus per turn versus card costs.
* **Space economy:** 20 slots in the grid, Spent cards clog them, Recall costs Focus, Stack cards reclaim slots or scale effects.
* **Activation economy:** Channel per turn versus activation limits and Charge thresholds.
* **Churn economy:** Insight per turn, Grimoire size, discard cycling.
* **Defense scaling:** Armor picked before the fight (one choice, no Focus), Shields that eat Channel every turn, Wards and Allies that eat Focus and slots, all against telegraphed enemy Tells.
* **Damage scaling:** Various ways to cause damage get multiplied with stronger decks due to synergies between cards, with strategic placement, and increasing stats with stronger items.

### 3.5 Victory and Defeat
* **Victory:** all enemies at 0 HP. Every card returns to the Grimoire, all Charges and Spent states clear, Burn, Poison, and Corrode are removed (3.3.1), and Insight, Focus, and Channel return to starting values. Rewards are granted per Section 6.1.
* **Defeat:** player HP reaches 0 with no immediate way to bring it back above 0 in that moment. The run ends.

## 4. Classes and Deck Archetypes

### 4.1 Classes
Three starting classes. Each has its own card pool plus a **shared pool** of common cards any class can find, so pools overlap at the edges. All three start with nearly the same Grimoire, the way every Slay the Spire character starts with Strikes and Defends. There is no starting element (3.3.2).

**Shared starting Grimoire (10 cards):** 4x **Blast** (Arcane, Attune, deal 4), 2x **Shield** (Arcane, Attune, gain 4 Guard), 1x **Familiar** (Ally, 6 HP), 1x **Clear Mind**, 1x **Focus Energy**, 1x class signature card. **Druid starting Grimoire (12 cards):** use four Shields instead of two, retaining four Blasts, Familiar, Clear Mind, Focus Energy, and the Druid signature Sapling.

| Class | Fantasy | Starting HP | Mechanical focus | Signature card |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Alchemist** | Change and adaptation | 60 | **Shift** (move placed cards to other slots), **Transmute** (change a placed card's element), cards that reposition themselves, re-Attuning Blasts and Shields mid-fight. | **Transmute** |
| **Druid** | Growth and pattern | 70 | Adjacency and pattern scaling, Pile and Fusion stacks, Allies that strengthen the longer they sit. | **Sapling** |
| **Sorcerer** | Raw spellpower | 55 | Charge cards, 2 and 3 Focus cards, Channel boosts. Fewer, bigger activations. | **Arcane Surge** |

**Shift** (Alchemist keyword): move one placed card to any empty slot. A stack moves as a unit. Locked cards cannot be Shifted.

**Every class has a nemesis.** Enemy decks include cards built to frustrate each class so no deck is safe everywhere:

* **Lock** punishes the Alchemist, whose plan is to keep rearranging.
* **Sever** punishes the Druid, whose cards are only as good as their neighbors.
* **Reflective Shield** and **Flicker** punish the Sorcerer, whose few activations are each expensive to waste.

The **Undead Fire Wolf** (5.2) is the Fire-focused Alchemist's nightmare: a Fire Eidolon with *Resist Fire* that Locks freshly placed cards and Severs one a turn.

### 4.2 Deck Archetypes
A Grimoire may specialize in one economy, several, or none. The safe baseline is decent numbers in all three, since a deck that cannot see, place, or activate enough will stall. Specialized decks trade that safety for a ceiling.

* **Channel deck:** piles on Channel boosts to activate the entire grid every turn. Loves high activation limits and Charge cards.
* **Focus deck:** piles on Focus boosts to flood the grid early and cash in pattern bonuses before the enemy ramps.
* **Insight deck:** sees most of the Grimoire every turn and relies on picking the exact card for the situation, with a thin, precise deck.
* **Stack deck:** ignores the economies and wins through Stack cards. A few slots each with low activation costs, and a pile of the same card combining damage. Plasma Ball (5.1) is the reference card. Its weakness is that the pile is one slot and one enemy effect away from disappearing, and its strongest turns require several cards with remaining uses in the pile.

Hybrid decks are expected; these are gravity wells, not classes.

Warn explicitly in the enemy description and battle card; visibly resolve the death attack before the Choir finishes disintegrating and the result/rewards appear.

## 6. Rewards and Progression

### 6.1 Victory Rewards
**Healing-card scarcity (v1):** Soothe, Bloomcall, Sunfruit, Unbinding Dew, and Aqua Veil use rare-only offers and one-quarter the selection weight of other rare cards, with no duplicate choices within one offer. This applies to battle rewards and Tavern stock. Each healing card has only a 25% chance to enter a fresh Item Deck; existing Item Decks, revealed offers, and acquired cards are retained when loading older saves. Warm Spring offers Rain Lantern instead of a guaranteed Soothe, while its direct recovery choice remains.

Every win grants **Gold** and a **card offer**. For ordinary battles, choose one of three cards to add to the Grimoire, or skip. After defeating an Archon, choose one of three distinct **legendary** cards; this choice is required before completing the Stratum or continuing to the next one. Harder or rarer fights add Gems and Settings or other items on top.

| Fight | Gold | Card offer | Gem / Setting |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mote (Wanderer, Stalker, Sentinel) | Small | 3 common cards | Rarely |
| **Skittish Mote** | Large | 3 cards, one rare | Often |
| Eidolon | Large | 3 cards, one rare | Always one Gem |
| Archon | Very large | 3 distinct legendary cards; choose one, no skip | One randomly determined Gem **and** one randomly determined Setting |

**Guaranteed legendary after each Archon.** Draw three distinct eligible legendary cards using the run seed, and preserve the offer across saving and resuming. Stratum 2 is balanced with the assumption that the player received at least one legendary at the end of Stratum 1. The v1 pool is Grove Titan, Eclipse Seed, and Heartwood Bastion; as the pool grows, offer a seeded selection of three without duplicates. Additional-enemy rarity bonuses cannot replace this guarantee or promote beyond legendary. Resolve any on-death attacks and survive the battle before granting victory rewards. In v1, collect the legendary before the Stratum 1 win screen.

**Battles with several enemies.** Gold is paid **per defeated enemy**, each at its own row's rate in the table above. Three Eidolons pay three Eidolon amounts; a Skittish Mote fought alongside an ordinary Mote pays one Skittish amount plus one ordinary amount. Only enemies that were on the Field when the battle began pay Gold; enemies summoned during the battle, such as Mini-Voids, pay nothing unless their card says otherwise, so summons cannot be farmed. The battle still gives **one card offer**, at the quality of the strongest enemy present, and every defeated Field enemy beyond the first **slightly improves it**: each extra enemy adds a small bonus, on the order of 5 percentage points and tuned by the builder, to the chance that each offered card rolls one rarity higher. Gem and Setting drops follow the strongest enemy present, with one roll per battle.

Skittish enemies are weaker fighters with richer drops, so chasing them is a deliberate detour worth taking. Sentinels are ordinary fights the player picks on their own schedule.

The Grimoire begins at about 10 generic cards and is expected to reach roughly 30 by the end of a run. Taverns let the player remove cards to keep it lean.

### 6.2 Equipment: Body Slots, Settings, and Gems
Equipment is deliberately shallow so the Mind Grid stays the source of complexity. The body has **seven slots**:

| Slot | Holds | Kind |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Head** | Crown or Helmet | Printed effect, no socket. |
| **Neck** | Necklace | **Setting:** printed effect plus one Gem socket. |
| **Torso** | Armor | Printed effect, no socket. Elemental Armor adjusts its remaining Guard using the same cycle as attacks. |
| **Left wrist, Right wrist** | Bracelets | **Setting:** printed effect plus one Gem socket. |
| **Left finger, Right finger** | Rings | **Setting:** printed effect plus one Gem socket. |

**Satchel capacity.** Ten shared loose-item slots apply under6.7, including consumables and spare equipment.

**Equipment timing.** During the player's Field movement phase, equipment can be changed before moving or between individual movement steps, without spending movement. Equipment changes remain available at Taverns. They are unavailable during battle, enemy movement, and unresolved Events or rewards. Socketing and unsocketing Gems are restricted to Taverns. The inventory has distinct **Equipped** and **Satchel** areas: equipped items appear only in Equipped, and socketed Gems stay with their Settings. Support dragging equipment in both directions to equip or unequip, as well as click controls. Swapping equipment returns the replaced item to Satchel. Unequipping retains the item and its socketed Gem; Cursed equipment remains locked until its removal requirements are met.

**Armor** and **Crowns** carry their own printed effects. The player may **carry up to 3 Armors** and wear one. Armor can be swapped only during the Field movement phase, never in battle, so choosing Armor is a read on the fight ahead. Armor protects the player only.

| Armor | Effect while worn |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fire Armor** (also Water, Earth, Wind) | Optional base2 Guard pool each enemy round: up to3 against the element it beats,1 against its weakness,2 otherwise. Earth Armor:3 against Wind,1 against Fire. Does not reduce ongoing status damage. |
| **Holy Armor** (Light) | Recover 2 HP on up to two injured player-turn starts per battle; full HP consumes no trigger. |
| **Stone Armor** | 30 Gold. Holds1 Guard, draining across hits and refilling each enemy turn; does not reduce ongoing status damage. |
| **Quicksilver Armor** | +1 tile of Field movement. No battle effect. |
| **Mirror Armor** | Choose to reflect an incoming enemy attack to its attacker, once per battle; may be skipped and saved for a later hit. |

**Settings** are the Necklace, Bracelets, and Rings. Each Setting has a **printed effect of its own** and **one Gem socket**. **Gems** are fully **interchangeable** among Settings: a Gem taken off a Ring can go into a Bracelet or the Necklace. A Gem does nothing until socketed. **Socketing and unsocketing happen only at Taverns.** Where a pairing would be game-breaking, the Setting simply says **"this Gem cannot be socketed here."**

* **Not every Gem has an element.** A Gem's effect is either an element or some other power.
* **An elemental Gem imbues the Setting** with its element. The Setting's numbers then follow the elemental defense rule (3.3.2) for defensive effects and the cycle for offensive ones. Imbued pieces also count as that element for upgrade conditions.
* **A non-elemental Gem adds its power** to the Setting's own.
* **Some Gem and Setting pairs are printed as synergistic or antagonistic**, or simply forbidden.

**Baseline set for v1.** The builder makes up the equipment catalog, and it can start very simple:

| Piece | Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Bronze / Silver / Gold Bracelet** | Guard 2 / 4 / 7, refilled every enemy turn, like an always-on Shield (3.3.3). |
| **Rootbound Ring** (starting Ring) | After the first damaging attack activation each player turn, one separate hit for 2 damage, using its socketed element (Arcane if none). |
| **Ruby / Sapphire / Emerald / Topaz** | Fire / Water / Earth / Wind (imbue). |

Imbue math on that baseline: a **Fire Gold Bracelet** absorbs 11 against Earth,4 against Water,7 against everything else including Fire (effective Guard rounds up). An **Earth Bronze Bracelet** absorbs 3 against Wind,1 against Fire (the element that beats Earth),2 against everything else including Earth. Rings, Necklaces, Crowns, non-elemental Gems (Insight, Channel, Focus, Field movement, Recall effects), and named pieces such as the Sturdy Metal Helmet from 2.9 come after the baseline works.

The player starts a run with one Bronze Bracelet equipped on the **right wrist**, one Rootbound Ring equipped on the **right finger**, and **one random elemental Gem**, which they socket into either the Bracelet or the Ring. The remaining Settings, Crowns, and Armors are found from Archons, Events, Item spawns, and Tavern shops.

**Rootbound Ring timing.** Its one bonus hit applies to the first target of the first activation that releases direct damage, including Ally attacks. Area attacks, multi-element attacks, Pile and Fusion do not multiply the trigger. Charge-building activations that release no damage and status-only effects do not spend it. A negated opening attack still spends it; if its target dies before the separate hit, the bonus does not retarget or carry forward. Each equipped Ring follows this rule independently; show Ready/Spent on it during battle. The opportunity refreshes next player turn and next battle, and remains spent across exact-state saves. Other equipment follows its own printed trigger.

**Selling.** Ordinary items can be sold at a Tavern for **half their worth**. Socketed Gems are directly sellable: unsocket the sold Gem and remove only that Gem from inventory. Selling a Setting automatically unsockets its Gem into the Satchel, preserving the exact Gem, and removes only the Setting; clear its equipment slot if equipped. Pay only for the sold item. A preserved Cursed Gem remains owned and subject to its Curse; selling its Setting does not remove the Curse. Cursed objects require a visiting Tavern Healer and a Gold removal fee instead of an ordinary sale (6.6).

### 6.3 Synthesis (future, not in v1)
Synthesis is a future feature, described in 11.3. **v1 builds do not implement it.** In v1, the way to make a card stronger is the optional upgrade (3.3).

### 6.4 Taverns
Heal, buy cards, Gems, Settings, and Armor, sell items for half their worth, remove a card from the Grimoire, socket and unsocket Gems, upgrade cards, pay to be rid of a Cursed object, sometimes see a Healer, and pay for gossip: revealing the Stratum's Archon, or hints about upcoming Events.

**Card purchase prices (v1).** Common cards cost **100 Gold**, rare cards **150 Gold**, and legendary cards **250 Gold** when offered for sale. Ward and Opening Rite each cost 100 Gold. These prices apply to card purchases only; equipment and Gem prices stay item-specific. The displayed price, affordability checks and actual debit must use the same price table. Existing acquired cards are retained; pending stock adopts the current prices when a saved run resumes.

**Current card balance (v1).** Solitude deals **10** base damage. Tide Memory grants **+1 Insight next turn per connected orthogonally adjacent occupied space**, counted when activated (0–4); covered cards and diagonal cards do not add to this count, and Sever interrupts the connection. It remains 0 Focus with two activations. Bitter Spore applies **Poison 2**, upgraded to **Poison 3**. Heat applies **Burn 2**, upgraded to **Burn 3**; its separate Fusion bonus remains Burn 6. Slow Rot applies **Corrode 1**; upgrading adds an immediate **3-damage Water hit**, with a rust-colored attack effect and normal elemental matchup rules, while Corrode stays 1. Patient Seed, Magnifying Glass Tower, Prismatic Core, Thorn Choir and Aqua Veil retain their current behavior.

**Current equipment balance (v1).** Ring of Patience costs **200 Gold**, Ring of Embers **240 Gold**, and Stone Armor **30 Gold**. Necklace of Dew heals 1 HP per trigger, or 2 with a Sapphire, with two actual healing triggers per battle as above. Husk Armor retains its 2-Guard pool and applies Corrode 1 at battle start. At the start of the player's fifth turn, before status damage, it converts the entire current Corrode value to an equal amount of Burn, adding to existing Burn and clearing Corrode. This conversion happens once per battle; Corrode applied afterward remains Corrode. A resumed older battle already past turn five converts on its next player-turn start. Existing healing saves without counters conservatively count elapsed player turns against each item's two-use allowance, avoiding a reload refill.

**Gold upgrade prices (v1).** Basic Blast and Shield upgrades cost **50 Gold**. Standard Gold-paid upgrades cost **100 Gold**: Ignis Spark, Water Blast, Gust, Bloomcall, Patient Seed, Slow Rot, and Bitter Spore. High-value damage upgrades cost **120 Gold**: Cinder Snap, Solitude, Thorn Choir, and Root Lance. Price by the value of the upgrade rather than deriving it from placement Focus; a zero-Focus attack can deserve a premium upgrade price. Existing special requirements remain: Sapling costs 8 HP, Storm Canopy sacrifices another rare card, and Heat requires a Fire-imbued Setting plus 60 Gold. These prices change costs only, not upgrade effects or card activation allowances. Already purchased upgrades remain purchased; unapplied upgrades use the current price, including on resumed runs.

**Store presentation.** Lay out shop stock and owned belongings as visual catalogs. Buy uses **Cards, Gems, Equipment** tabs; Sell / remove uses **Grimoire, Equipment** tabs (the latter includes Gems). Show one category at a time instead of stacked dropdowns and retain its selection after transactions. Cursed objects and Hexes appear grayed out in these catalogs, with disabled transaction controls and the text **"Requires a Tavern Healer to remove."** Keep View available. Cursed objects and Hexes cannot be bought or removed through the market; removal is performed at an available Healer. Outline currently equipped items in the sell catalog and label them **Equipped**, including equipped Cursed objects. List socketed Gems as separate normal sale entries, label them **Equipped**, and name their Setting; retain View and Sell without requiring manual unsocketing. Every purchasable item or card has separate View and Buy controls; every sellable item has View and Sell controls. View shows the full artwork, effects, price, and applicable restrictions before any transaction. Display the Grimoire visually for card inspection and removal. Items sell for Gold at their normal sale value; cards are not sold for Gold. Ordinary paid card removal is limited to one card per Tavern visit; show when the service has been used. Healer Hex removal is separate and does not consume this allowance. The Scribe uses the same visual card catalog style, showing every owned card with artwork, View and Upgrade controls. Explain upgrade benefits on hover and show why unavailable upgrades are disabled. Keep Hexes grayed out with the Healer-removal tooltip. Ordinary card removal appears only in the Market, not as a duplicate Scribe service.

### 6.5 Complexity Budget
The target is Slay the Spire's level of deckbuilding, not Arkham Horror's. The Mind Grid already adds a placement economy and a space economy on top of activation and reveal. Everything outside battle is therefore kept flat: seven equipment slots, sockets, one-line Gems, one-line Armors with a carry limit of three, and a card offer of three. Defense is deliberately not a player stat: it is an Armor choice and grid slots spent on Allies and Wards. Elements are a single rule (add half forward, take half backward) and enemy resistances are printed on the enemy card rather than kept in a table. Any new system should be paid for by simplifying one of these.

### 6.6 Curses and Hexes
Two kinds of bad luck, one per layer of the game:

* A **Curse** is always a **Cursed object**: a Cursed Ring, a Cursed Gem, a Cursed Armor. Its text either **forces the player to equip it** or **penalizes them while it is not equipped**. A Cursed object cannot be dropped. The only way out is to **pay a visiting Tavern Healer to remove it**, or an Event that removes it.
* A **Hex** is a bad **card**. It sits in the Grimoire and is revealed like any card. **Hexes may cause any negative effect, including direct damage.** Each Hex names a zone where it bites (the discard pile, the reveal, the Grimoire as a whole, or the grid) and usually offers an expensive way to manage it that costs Focus, Channel, or a slot. Some Hexes can be placed, some cannot.

| Hex | Bites while... | Penalty | Placement | Activation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bone Lock** | in the **discard pile** | Focus is halved, rounded down, minimum 1. | 2 Focus. *Isolated.* | None. Placing it is the only way to keep it out of the discard pile, and it then clogs a slot. |
| **Milky Eyes** | **revealed** this turn | The next card placed this turn costs +1 Focus. | Cannot be placed. | None. It is discarded with the other unchosen cards and returns on the next reshuffle. |
| **Burning Itch** | anywhere in the **Grimoire, discard, or grid** | Lose 1 HP at the start of every battle turn. | 2 Focus. | Unlimited. **Soothing Relief:** the HP loss is suppressed for the next 5 turns. Costs a slot, 2 Focus, and 1 Channel every 5 turns. |

**Getting them.** Events are the main source. Some Events offer a strong reward with a Curse attached. Losing a battle does not curse the player; the run simply ends.

**Removing them.** Events and Taverns. A visiting Tavern **Healer** is required to remove either a Cursed object or a Hex; the Market cannot remove them. A Tavern may or may not have a Healer. Cursed-object removal costs Gold. Removing a Hex may require **Gold, HP only, HP plus Gold, an Ally card plus Gold, or an item**. Display the full payment before the player chooses; the player selects any card or item to sacrifice, which is permanently removed. An HP payment cannot reduce the player to zero HP. Hex removal is separate from the once-per-Tavern ordinary card-removal service. Cursed-object removal retains its Gold fee. Treatment requirements vary and some Healers **gouge**; the gossip line tells the player whether the next Tavern has one. Some Events inflict a Curse with a **quest** attached: the next Event that spawns is guaranteed to be the side quest that, if completed, removes that Curse or Hex.

### 6.7 Consumables and the Satchel (v1)

**Consumables** are finite items carried in the Satchel, not cards or equipment. Using one permanently removes that exact bottle from the run; it does not return next battle. They cost **no Channel**, but the player may use **at most one consumable per player turn**, shared across Placement and Activation. The allowance refreshes at the next player-turn start, not on a phase arrow, save/load, Recall, or enemy hit. Consumables cannot interrupt enemy movement, enemy attacks, defense choices, status damage, Reveal, or death resolution. Healing Sap can also be used during player Field movement, at most once per movement round, without spending movement. Entering a battle starts that battle's independent allowance.

| Consumable | Immediate effect | Timing | Tavern price |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | ---: |
| **Healing Sap** | Restore **5 HP**, capped at maximum. Unavailable at full HP; partial healing still consumes it. | Placement, Activation, or player Field movement | 25 Gold |
| **Focus Draught** | Gain **1 Focus** this turn. | Placement only | 35 Gold |
| **Channel Draught** | Gain **1 Channel** this turn. Existing per-card activation limits still apply. | Activation only | 40 Gold |
| **Insight Dew** | Gain and immediately spend **1 Insight** to draw one extra card now; preserve the existing hand and phase. Recycle discard normally if needed; unavailable when no card can be drawn. | Placement only | 25 Gold |
| **Star Flask** | Deal **6 Arcane damage** to one chosen living enemy, using normal enemy Guard, resistance and damage rules. | Activation only | 30 Gold |

Temporary resources are not permanent increases; unused bonus Focus or Channel expires with the turn. Insight Dew uses the run's seeded draw RNG. Star Flask is an item attack, not a card activation: it does not trigger Rings, other activation-linked equipment attacks, or use a card's allowance. Boss reactions to matching-element damage still apply; killing Glass Choir still resolves Final Note before victory. Do not skip damage, death or reward animations.

**Starting Field supplies.** At the start of each Stratum, place **three visible consumables on seeded random tiles**, including exactly one Healing Sap and two distinct selections from the other four supplies. These are optional destinations, not starting inventory. Spread the three in different directions; one should be reasonably accessible and two require a detour. Current v1 placement uses one at Chebyshev distance2–3 from the center and two at distance4–5, in separated directions. Use distinct tiles, excluding the player and all occupants of the first spawn pair. These supplies are additional to the normal16pairs, do not consume queue entries or advance Restlessness, and never decay or expire. Preserve positions and identities on save/load. Existing in-progress Strata are not retroactively populated; their current inventories and offers remain unchanged. Later Strata should use this same starting-supply rule when implemented.

Show each actual bottle with a readable effect marker; clicking inspects its identity and full effect even from far away. For a reachable supply, offer an explicit Move here control as well as the directional movement buttons. Arrival forfeits remaining movement as for other pickups. Offer **Collect**, **Drink now** when legal, or **Leave it here**. Leaving one of these visible starting supplies preserves it for a later visit and does not immediately reopen the pickup on the same visit; returning after moving away offers it again. This is an explicit exception to ordinary hidden Item-spawn forfeiture. Healing Sap may be drunk directly from any item pickup while injured and within the once-per-movement-round allowance, even with a full Satchel; it restores up to5HP, is permanently consumed, and is recorded as a pickup use. Other supplies remain battle-only. Collecting into a full Satchel pauses progression for the normal explicit capacity choice before further enemy movement. An enemy occupying a supply tile is fought before the supply can be collected or used. Healing is a limited recovery destination, not a safe zone or a pause to pursuit.

**Availability and controls.** Also include these five items in the normal Item Deck. Every newly generated normal Tavern adds two distinct consumable offers to its other stock; purchases are finite, without restocking. Existing revealed item offers, decks and shop stock are not rerolled during migration. Buy and Sell each have a Consumables tab with View/full art, effects and price; normal half-worth selling applies. Show a persistent compact consumable tray on the Field and in battle, with visible counts, usable/disabled states and hover reasons. Copies may share a tray icon for readability but remain separate owned items. Clicking the image inspects it. Clicking a use control commits a targetless use. Star Flask's throw control highlights legal enemies; clicking a highlighted enemy or dragging the bottle onto it commits. Escape, Cancel, clicking elsewhere or dropping off-target spends nothing.

**Ten shared Satchel slots.** Each loose consumable, loose Gem or spare equipment item uses **one** slot, including every duplicate bottle. Equipped gear and socketed Gems use none; an unequipped Setting with a socketed Gem occupies just the Setting's one slot. Cards occupy the Grimoire and use no Satchel space. Preserve the existing three-Armor limit. Equipping into an empty body slot can free space; unequipping, unsocketing, or selling/trading a Setting may return items to the Satchel. Show used/10 in Inventory.

If an acquisition or inventory change would leave more than ten loose items, preserve all items and stop ordinary progression at an explicit keep/discard choice. Let the player discard an eligible loose item, including the new acquisition, or free space through a legal use/equipment change. Explain that discard is permanent and pays no Gold. Discarding a Setting returns its socketed Gem to the Satchel. Never silently discard a prior item or reroll the reward. Cursed items and Settings carrying Cursed Gems cannot be discarded to evade removal rules. If protected Cursed belongings alone exceed capacity, retain them rather than deadlocking the run; capacity enforcement resumes when ordinary discardable items are present. Resolve an already pending enemy hit before showing a migrated overflow choice.

**Persistence, AI and records.** Save every item's identity and the current turn's consumable-use marker. Exact-state archives and tutorial saves resume those values. Normal Continue retains its existing battle-start restart rule: both battle progress and consumable inventory return together to the same checkpoint, never HP alone. Migrate old saves without losing belongings; overfull inventories require an explicit choice at the next safe decision. The AI receives legal use actions with concrete effects, costs and targets, plus capacity and discard actions; it must be able to use, buy and make space for consumables. Archive consumable use, targets, actual HP restored, turn, movement round and rules/content versions for analysis.

## 7. Decisions

### 7.1 Confirmed
1. Player starts at the True Center (5,5). A run is four Strata with an automatic Tavern between them; winning is defeating the Stratum 4 Archon.
2. Field movement is 2 tiles per round. Items, Spells, and Armor may raise it permanently or for one round; Curses may lower it. Armor swaps happen in this phase.
3. Unchosen revealed cards and Recalled cards go to the discard pile, which is shuffled into a new Grimoire once the Grimoire is empty.
4. A Stratum is 16 pairs (32 spawns), with two spawns per round and the next four pairs visible. Its only Field Tavern is in pair 8 and the Archon in pair 16. Other spawns use the two-stage weighted rolls in 2.2. The Archon spawns at the True Center and uses its printed waiting and pursuit behavior, tuned so the fight is almost impossible to avoid within six Field rounds of its appearance (2.6).
5. Enemy battle actions can target the Mind Grid. Every such effect uses a named, deterministic targeting rule with reading-order tiebreaks (3.2). No random card selection.
6. The v3 Base/Enhancement two-layer system is replaced by the Stack keyword, including Levels and Towers.
7. Random Field movement reuses two Location dice (one per axis). No d8 required.
8. Enemies may step onto anything on the Field, including other enemies. An enemy standing on Gold, an Item, a Tavern, or an Event guards it: fight first, then collect or enter. Enemies sharing a tile fight together.
9. All enemies on the player's tile at the end of the enemy movement phase fight as one battle. During player movement, stepping onto an enemy starts the battle at once. Pack Movement N (group) triggers extra movement toward the player for packmates when any packmate reaches the player.
10. Hunters have unlimited range.
11. Seven body slots: head, neck, torso, two wrists, two fingers. No weapons or shields.
12. Gems replace the earlier Artifact concept. A socketed Gem is this game's relic.
13. Settings are the Necklace, Bracelets, and Rings. Each has a printed effect and exactly one Gem socket. Gems are fully interchangeable among Settings except where a Setting says a Gem cannot be socketed there. Crowns and Armor have printed effects and no socket.
14. Socketing and unsocketing happen only at Taverns.
15. Stack defaults: only the top card of a slot activates, and Recalling a slot costs the sum of the Recall costs inside it. Individual cards override both.
16. The element cycle is Fire -> Earth -> Wind -> Water -> Fire, with +50% forward and -50% backward.
17. Chaos and Light sit outside the cycle and deal +50% to each other. There is no Undead trait.
18. No starting element. Blast and Shield are Arcane starting cards that Attune to an adjacent element.
19. "Guard" is stored defensive capacity, used by Shields, Bracelets, numeric Armor and enemies; Wards retain their persistent ward value. It replaces the former player-facing resource term throughout cards, items, enemy descriptions, UI and tutorials. A Shield absorbs 4 for one turn, with two total activations per placement and at most one per turn unless Blink applies. A Bracelet is an always-on Shield whose Guard refills every enemy turn. Wards hold a "ward value" instead. Elemental Guard and Armor protection use the offensive cycle: +50% against the element they beat, -50% against their weakness; same-element and Arcane matchups are neutral. Round effective protection up.
20. Attacks traverse chosen Wards, Shields, or Allies in the same column or toward the player, never backward toward the enemy. Armor and Bracelets share the last equipment position, then HP; each hit resets its route. Clicking the Druid skips all unused defenses, including Armor.
21. Wards and Shields are optional, independently tracked defenses with no type priority or FIFO order. Click an eligible card to defend; multiple portions on that card are selected beside it. Take hit preserves all unused defenses.
22. The player may choose an eligible Ally at any positional defense choice, all-or-nothing. Overflow can continue to another eligible defense closer to the player or in the same column, unless swallowed (Energy Guardian).
23. One Armor worn at a time, carry up to 3, swap only during the Field movement phase.
24. Starting HP varies by class and averages about 60.
25. A Curse is a bad item, a Hex is a bad card. Removed by Events or a Tavern Healer. Some Events attach a Curse with a guaranteed follow-up side quest Event that removes it.
26. Restlessness (2.8) as written: one token per What-dice batch, +1 Field movement and +1 attack damage each.
27. Sever as written, including that it satisfies Isolated.
28. Adjacent means the four orthogonal neighbors unless a card says otherwise.
29. Damage cards hit one enemy of the player's choice unless they say all enemies or random enemies. No player-distributed damage.
30. An attack's element is printed on its Tell; it usually matches the enemy or is Arcane, but need not.
31. Charge resets on Recall. Turns end explicitly. No Focus or Channel carry-over unless a card or item grants it.
32. Card rarity is common, rare, legendary. Upgrades exist for some cards, not all, some with special conditions.
33. Synthesis is a v2 feature (one Composite per element pair, made during the Field movement phase, consuming both cards). It is not in v1. v1 has optional upgrades on a minority of cards, with requirements printed on each card.
34. Taverns stay on the Field until visited, then close. Gold and Items sit on the Field indefinitely; Gold decays 5 per round.
35. Items come from an Item Deck; Events from a per-Stratum Event Deck.
36. The player starts with one Bracelet, one Ring, and one random elemental Gem socketed into whichever of the two the player chooses.
37. Herald appears on roughly one in five Motes and one in three Eidolons per Stratum deck.
38. Attune picks one adjacent element at activation unless the card says otherwise. Cards that reward all four elements adjacent say so literally; there is no keyword.
39. Per-class starting HP of 60, 70, 55 for Alchemist, Druid, Sorcerer. New Druid runs start at 70/70; existing saves retain their current and maximum HP.
40. Archon pacing (2.6) is a guideline, not a hard mechanic: a few rounds of freedom, then the fight is almost impossible to avoid within six Field rounds. The spawn round counts as round one; battle turns do not advance the count.
41. A Curse is always a Cursed object (Ring, Gem, Armor) that forces equipping or penalizes not equipping. It cannot be dropped; removing it requires a visiting Tavern Healer and costs Gold. Ordinary items sell for half their worth.
42. Gold value: two spare d6 (0-5 faces) summed, times 10, minimum 10.
43. Not every Gem has an element. An elemental Gem imbues its Setting with that element; a non-elemental Gem adds its power. Armor is not a Setting and takes no Gem.
44. Upgrades happen at Taverns and some Events, priced in an equal-rarity card or Gold, with a possible class discount from a sorcerer NPC.
45. Howl stays as one of the Wolf's Tells.
46. The final Archon is the Apex.
47. A Healer can remove a Hex for a displayed Gold, HP, Ally-card, or item payment (6.6), separately from ordinary card removal. Cursed-object removal also requires a visiting Healer and costs Gold.
48. Upgrade costs are printed per card with flavor text: destroy an equal-rarity card, destroy two lesser cards, take on clogging cards, pay Gold, or lose HP once. Sorcerer discount is small but noticeable.
49. Rounding: damage dealt or absorbed rounds up; resources round down with a floor of 1 unless a card explicitly says 0.
50. The equipment catalog is the builder's to invent, starting from bronze, silver, and gold bracelets and the four elemental Gems.
51. The element is called Wind, never Air.

**Every enemy needs a designed move cycle, not just numbers.** Treat enemies the way Slay the Spire does: each one is a small puzzle with a personality, a fixed and readable rotation of Tells, and at least one mechanic that changes how the player has to play against it. For each enemy, write down:

* **Field behavior:** movement type and distance, Pack Movement group if any, Restlessness feel.
* **Battle rotation:** the ordered list of Tells it cycles through, with attack values and elements printed per Tell. Rotations may branch on a condition (below half HP, when an Ally is on the grid, every third turn) but must stay deterministic.
* **Signature mechanic:** the one thing that makes this enemy different. A grid attack with a deterministic target rule, a status it inflicts, a buff it stacks on itself, a rule it bends (Reflective Shield, Flicker, Resist), a summon, a phase change.
* **Counterplay:** one sentence on what a player is supposed to do about it. If there is no answer, the enemy is not finished.
* **Nemesis role:** which class or deck style it is meant to punish, if any.

Aim for variety across the roster: some enemies ramp and must be killed fast, some punish sitting still, some punish a crowded grid, some punish an empty one, some are safe to ignore on the Field and some are not. Archons should read as a fight with a beginning, a middle, and an end, not a big Mote.

### 10.5 The Game Must Be Playable by an AI
This is a hard requirement, not a nice-to-have. The long-term purpose is to find broken strategies and unfair mechanics before a human does.

* The rules engine must run **headless**, with no UI, driven by a program that sees exactly what a human player would see and chooses from exactly the actions a human could take at that moment.
* Every run must be **seedable and replayable**: same seed, same choices, same outcome.
* **The AI player** is a static, rule-based bot with a naive starting configuration. It does not need to be good. It must make real decisions rather than random ones, be able to give a one-line templated reason for each, and be built so that it **can learn over time given the action space at any point in the game**: the decision layer must be swappable for something trained later without touching the engine.
* Provide a **batch mode**. For this test, the batch is **5 runs**, as proof that unattended play and reporting work. In the future the same batch mode will run **100 runs**; nothing in its design should assume a small number.

**Per-run report**, one entry per run:
1. Win or loss.
2. Why, in a short causal explanation, not a log dump.
3. Whether the outcome looks like **luck** (dice, draws, spawn order), **strategy** (a plan that would work again), or a **broken mechanic** (something the rules allow that no designer intended).
4. If a broken mechanic: name it, show the sequence that exploits it, and **suggest a fix** in rules terms.

**Aggregate report.** For v1, the building LLM reads the bot's run logs and writes the interpretation itself. With 5 naive runs there may be little to glean, and that is fine. Something basic is the target: "enemies at the start do far too much damage," or "the starting cards are far too strong," or "starting balance feels about right," with the evidence that led there. The report format should scale to 100 runs and to win rate by class later.

#### 10.5.1 AI Interface, Robustness, and Future Training
The AI chooses from the actions available at each decision point using the current player-visible state. The engine owns legality and rule resolution; the bot must not maintain a separate copy of the game rules.

* **Engine-owned decisions.** Provide the observation, relevant player-visible history, and the complete set of currently legal choices, including their parameters. Cover every player decision: Field movement, equipment, placement, activation, Recall, Shield selection, Ally interception, Taverns, rewards, and Events. Submitting a choice advances the engine to the next decision or a terminal result. The human interface and bot use the same rules and legal actions.
* **Structured actions.** Describe choices through their action type, parameters, costs, targets, and public effects. The policy evaluates the candidates supplied by the engine rather than assuming a fixed list of card names or action IDs. Shared features should let new cards and familiar effects be evaluated without card-specific code. Descriptions must not reveal hidden outcomes.
* **Robustness to new choices.** Modest rule or content changes and unfamiliar legal choices must not crash the bot or cause illegal moves. Use a valid fallback when the policy cannot evaluate a choice; do not silently omit unfamiliar choices from the available action set. If there is no player decision, the engine advances or reports completion rather than requiring a fabricated action. Continuing to function is required; playing genuinely new mechanics well may require new features and further training. Validate this behavior with representative altered rules or unfamiliar actions.
* **Replaceable decision policy.** v1 uses a rule-based bot with configurable starting weights for generally useful, context-sensitive considerations such as survival, threat removal, resource use, grid development, and progress toward victory. Preserve its one-line decision explanations. The interface must allow later replacement with weight optimization, a learned policy, or bounded Monte Carlo tree search guided by a weighted or learned policy, without rewriting the rules engine. Search chooses actions; training improves the policy or evaluation across runs.
* **Equipment competence.** The baseline must evaluate all current equipment effects, including reflection, Shield activation bonuses, Recall discounts, trigger limits, socketed benefits and item synergies. Prefer beneficial gear in empty legal slots and compare net benefits when replacing gear; account for curses and drawbacks, and avoid pointless equip/unequip loops. Unknown effects must retain legal fallbacks. Verify missed-effect fixes with focused tests and matching-seed baseline comparisons, retaining versioned traces; do not infer human balance from bot losses caused by evaluation gaps.
* **Fair future simulation.** Any later search uses isolated simulations and samples plausible hidden outcomes from information available to the player. It must not inspect the actual secret random state, upcoming draws, unrevealed Archon, or other hidden facts. Simulation must not alter the live run or its random sequence. Deterministic replay does not grant the bot knowledge of the hidden future.
* **Versioned learning and evaluation.** Record rules/content versions, observation/action interface versions, and policy configuration with run logs. Future training must associate checkpoints with those versions, reuse compatible knowledge, and permit continued training on new game versions. Flag incompatible data or checkpoints rather than silently treating them as compatible. Evaluate trained policies against a baseline on seeds excluded from training. Exact replay requires the corresponding rules/content version.
* **v1 boundary.** Deliver the working weighted bot, the robust decision interface, versioned logs, and the executed five-run evaluation required by 10.5. Monte Carlo search, weight optimization, learned models, and a training pipeline are future capabilities, not additional v1 completion requirements. Five runs demonstrate unattended play and reporting; they do not establish a well-trained AI or a balanced game.

### 11.1 Difficulty levels
Multiple difficulties are a v2 feature. v1 has one difficulty, which becomes the easiest, with **two spawns per Field round**. Harder difficulties increase this to **three, then four spawns per round**; the hardest also raises the probability of monsters relative to Items and Gold. Keep 16 spawning rounds (48 or 64 total spawns), a preview of the next four groups, the midpoint Tavern, and the final-round Archon. Exact harder-mode weights and shorter Archon catch windows remain future tuning; an approximately two-turn catch window is one candidate. The goal is a crowded center that makes movement and positioning consequential. Restlessness remains tied to elapsed four-round blocks rather than the number of individual arrivals.

### 11.2 Encounter caps (tentative)
Two simultaneous Eidolons may be unfair, but neither a total enemy cap nor a one-Eidolon cap is approved. Evaluate combinations of damage, denial, and Pack Movement, not just enemy counts. Any future cap must define what happens to excess enemies and whether sequential battles permit recovery or rewards between them; do not silently discard enemies or impose a cap.

### 11.3 Synthesis
During the Field movement phase the player merges two cards of different elements into one **Composite** card that counts as both elements and uses whichever multiplier is better against the target. There is **one Composite per element pair**, each designed individually, for example **Steam** (Fire + Water): deal 5 damage and every Ally recovers 2 HP. Synthesis is **recipe-based**: players discover recipes over time; arbitrary pairs cannot merge without a matching recipe. Each recipe defines its ingredients and result, including costs, effects, activation allowance, and upgrade behavior. Recipe discovery and persistence across runs are builder decisions to record for v2. Synthesis consumes both cards unless a card says otherwise.

Or: must have fought and beaten a specific rare Skittish mote at some point.  Or, must have encountered a specific event paying a beggar 200 gold while receiving nothing in return. ### 11.5 Corruptions: statuses attached to spaces (Act 2 onward)

**Corruptions attach to Mind Grid spaces, not to cards, enemies or the player.** Enemies place them as hostile effects beneath the player's card stacks. Neither Corruptions nor their dedicated counter-cards belong in Act 1 content pools. The v2 build implements them in Stratum 2 (10.12). Upgrades unlock harder repairs. Detailed proposals and new enemy concepts live in [Stratum 2 planning](stratum_2_planning.md).

A Corruption stays on its space when a card is placed, Recalled, destroyed or moved. Covering can suppress an effect where its text says so; it does not remove the Corruption. Keep a distinct space-status symbol visible even while covered, with full hover details and any value/countdown. Telegraph upcoming placements so the player has a preparation turn. An ordinary card-status cleanse must not implicitly remove a space status; removal requires an effect that explicitly addresses Corruptions.

### 11.6 Later-act bosses and displacement

**Spreading Wildfire (Act 3 concept).** A later boss can destroy a targeted card and recursively spread destruction through all orthogonally connected occupied spaces. A connected formation can therefore be wiped across the entire grid; empty spaces separate safe clusters. This is distinct from Cinder Hart's Act 1 row-destruction move. Encourage separated clusters while preserving a reason to seek adjacency bonuses. Telegraph the starting target and the entire threatened connected group for one player turn. Exact target selection, covered-stack treatment and rotation remain future design work.

**Third Act 3 boss — Vertical Shear / Horizontal Shear.** These moves test reliance on fixed card positions. Start from the fullest column or row, counting every card, including covered cards. Use the existing deterministic ties (leftmost column / topmost row). Affect exactly that line and the next two consecutive lines, wrapping the selected line indices past the grid edge.

* **Horizontal Shear:** shift all stacks in the selected row right by 1 space; shift the following row right by 2; shift the third row right by 3. Positions wrap modulo the number of columns.
* **Vertical Shear:** shift all stacks in the selected column down by 1 space; shift the following column down by 2; shift the third column down by 3. Positions wrap modulo the number of rows.
* **Confirmed:** move whole stacks, preserving internal order and card state. Corruptions remain on their original spaces. Resolve each line as a simultaneous cyclic shift, including empty spaces, rather than sequential pushes or merges. Moving a cover can expose a Corruption and the incoming stack can cover another. Recompute adjacency and positional effects after movement.
* For width W and height H, Horizontal Shear beginning at row r maps each (x, (r+k) mod H) to ((x+k+1) mod W, (r+k) mod H), for k = 0,1,2. Vertical Shear beginning at column c maps each ((c+k) mod W, y) to ((c+k) mod W, (y+k+1) mod H), for k = 0,1,2.
* Example on 9×6: choosing row 6 shifts rows 6, 1, 2 right by 1, 2, 3. Choosing column 9 shifts columns 9, 1, 2 down by 1, 2, 3. Display movement arrows and destinations before execution and animate the displacement. The boss's name, full rotation/stats, exact telegraph commitment, and interactions with Locked cards or unplayable Memory Hole spaces remain future decisions.

### 11.7 Mind Grid expansion and later-act evaluation

**The current playtest build now uses a 7-column × 6-row Mind Grid (42 spaces), including Act 1.** Jonathan approved this expansion from 5×4 to evaluate room for Act 2 Corruptions and later spatial mechanics. Apply all existing placement, orthogonal adjacency, corners, rows/columns, 2×2 formations, Towers and telegraphs across the full board. Keep starting Insight/Focus/Channel, deck size and card/enemy values unchanged for the initial comparison. Existing saves retain stacks at their original row/column coordinates with new empty spaces added to the right and below; old edge/corner positions follow the expanded board's geometry. Preserve the horizontal 16:9 layout, full-board visibility, phase controls, bottom hand, player/equipment and enemy Tells. Make compact card values readable and retain click-for-full-details. The earlier 9×6 proposal remains a possible future experiment, not the current requirement. Corruptions and their counter-cards remain Act 2+ content and are not added to v1 by this grid-size change.

Evaluate viable formations and response opportunities, not simply empty-space count. Extra space makes isolation and separate elemental clusters easier and lowers pressure to Recall/stack, while a larger board does not increase Focus or the number of placements. Effects counting entire rows can scale while four-neighbor effects retain the same maximum. Rebalance line attacks, hazards and content against the chosen dimensions. Preserve readability within the horizontal 16:9 layout: fitting 9×6 into the current grid footprint would make cells about 44% narrower and 33% shorter. Do not shrink readable card information into illegibility. The 7×6 grid experiment is explicitly approved for the current v1 build; all Act 2/3 mechanics remain future work.
